# Destination Research

Read `../../workflows/research-protocol.md` and `../../../AGENTS.md`. Read `local/trip.json`, then claim exactly one eligible destination through `scripts/research.mjs`. At the start of the execution, choose the running researcher model and export `TRAVEL_RESEARCHER_MODEL` (or `TRAVEL_MODEL`); record the choice as `configuredModel` (`.agents/config/researcher.json` is only the fallback default). Take execution mode from the actual host invocation. Configuration is not proof of actual model identity; keep `model` and `modelEvidence` null unless runtime evidence is available. Do not require `local/automation.json`. Never access portfolio or personal wiki during travel research.

Write an evidence-backed dossier, not a generic travel guide. Assess all origin airports and date windows; deeply price at least one exact-date option for both travelers and explain rejected routes/windows. Save rendered browser text with exact search context. Separate observed party totals from estimates, include all hotel nights and fees, baggage, meals, transfers, activities and contingency. Recheck airline-direct totals without buying. Record alternative flight/hotel combinations and why the preferred combination wins.

Every dossier provides explained qualitative confidence and conditional value assessments for its options, unresolved flags, reviewer findings and prioritized user actions with why/how to verify them and what would change the itinerary or recommendation. Publish validated privacy-safe research automatically after the review attempt, including estimates and provisional status. Do not hide the dossier because clean approval or booking readiness is missing. Keep unsupported claims qualified and private trip details in the local portal. Follow AGENTS.md Decision-Support Publication.

Use assets/dossier-template.json for the dossier extension to the existing report envelope. Every section needs text, public sources, run-local evidence and observedAt. Write a complete date-by-date itinerary referencing a priced candidate, including outbound and return travel, lodging, transfer time and rainy-day alternatives. Research official entry/transit and US re-entry constraints for each traveler. Unknown documents or remote-work permission prevent buy recommendations, not honest conditional analysis.

Distinguish regional seasonal climatology from forecasts; discuss daylight, rain, sea conditions, altitude, winter closures, Christmas/New Year schedules and capacity. Assess neighborhoods, experience quality, crowding, flight fatigue, cancellation exposure, budget sensitivity, who would dislike the destination and the strongest competing itinerary.

## Itinerary Depth

Every non-travel day needs a narrative plan plus at least two visits, each with its own reason to earn time: what the place is famous for (`significance`), why this trip spends time there (`whyVisit`), how long to allow (`duration`), seasonal/holiday hours, booking and transport specifics (`logistics`), and the source-backed cost or explicit unknown (`cost`). Beach/rest days still name the beach or activity, how to get there, and what it costs. The `plan` field must read as the day's story (morning/afternoon/evening flow), not a one-line label. Travel-only days use `travelDayReason` and empty visits. Every visit needs its own public source and run-local evidence; a day without evidence is a sketch, not research.

## Visa Process Detail

`dossier.entry` is a how-to-apply guide for an Indian passport holder, not a verdict. Write numbered `steps` in the official application order (at least three), a `documents` checklist (at least three specific items: exact forms, photos, statements, fees proof), `fees` with amounts per person, `whereToApply` naming the exact channel usable from the SF Bay Area (online portal URL, VFS center, consulate address and appointment booking page) taken from official pages, plus `leadTimeDays`, `lastSafeApplyDate` counted back from departure, and `feasibleByDepart`. Anything unknown gets a `nextAction` naming the exact check. Never guess timelines; never cite visa-agency blogs over government, embassy or official e-visa pages.

Checkpoint only after scripts/publish.mjs validation succeeds. Hand off to travel-review; do not self-approve. Retain partial evidence after a blocker, with next action and retry date. Cite weak or missing evidence honestly. No invented price, image, review attempt or runtime identity.

## Funnel Scope And Narrative

Every configured destination gets its own end-to-end run (`.agents/workflows/decision-funnel.md`). The goal is the best possible trip for this location, argued on its own merits, not shaped around other destinations. Read `local/decision-profile.json`: US re-entry, remote work, the firm USD 7,000 ceiling and the 24-hour travel cap are user-attested constraints, cited as such. Entry authorization for an Indian passport holder remains a researched fact with a lead time and a last safe application date.

The structured fields are the evidence ledger. Also write `dossier.memo` ({ headline, markdown }) following `.agents/skills/advisor-memo/SKILL.md`: first-person advisor voice, recommendation first, itinerary as narrative chapters explaining why each base and stop matters, real cost arithmetic, the strongest alternative, and what to do this week. A dossier without a memo fails checkpoint validation.

Three more structured fields are required and validated at checkpoint:

- `dossier.entry`: the entry route for an Indian passport holder who holds a valid US visa (`mechanism`, `requirement`, `usVisaDependency`, `transit`, `fees`, an official `source`). State `leadTimeDays` and `lastSafeApplyDate` counted back from the departure date, and `feasibleByDepart`. Also list `steps` (the official application order, at least three) and `documents` (what to prepare, from the official list), so the user can act on the report without leaving it. Eligibility answers from the official checker (nationality, purpose, family links) belong in `requirement`. A visa is a process to finish on time, not a hard bound: report the lead time and what it takes, and only mark `feasibleByDepart` false when the timeline genuinely cannot be met from today. Use the government, embassy or official e-visa page, never a visa-agency blog. If processing time is not published, set `leadTimeDays` to null, `feasibleByDepart` to null and write a `nextAction`; never guess true. Include any transit-country requirement for the routing you priced. Today's date matters: if the last safe date has passed or is within days, say so first in the memo.
- `dossier.lodging`: two to four stays, each `mid-range`, `upper-mid-range` or `local-boutique`, with `area`, `whyHere`, a `guestRating` ({score, scale, source}), `nightlyUsd` with `priceBasis`, and public `sources`. Prefer places with strong recent guest ratings in a walkable or well-connected area; include a well-regarded independent local property when one exists. The user holds a Chase Sapphire Reserve and wants stays that can use its hotel benefits, so for each property set `chaseTravel` to `the-edit`, `chase-travel-portal`, `not-listed` or `unverified` with a `chaseNote`. Use only Chase's public pages to decide this. The Chase Travel portal requires the user's own login, so never sign in; if the status cannot be seen publicly, use `unverified` and say the user should check the portal. Do not state card benefits as fact unless a public Chase page shows them, do not subtract benefit value from cost, and do not let a card match outrank a clearly better stay.
- `dossier.travelerVoices`: three to six real voices (`author`, `context` such as "blog, 2025", `takeaway`, `url`, optional `quote`). Use independent sources from at least two different sites: travel blogs, forum threads, local food writers, recent review text. Paraphrase in `takeaway`; a `quote` is optional and must be 30 words or fewer. Choose voices that confirm, complicate or contradict the official picture, especially about late December crowds, closures, weather and value. Never invent a voice or an author.

Do not write generic travel-guide prose. Each section must be able to change the user's decision or the itinerary; otherwise cut it. Weave `travelerVoices` into the memo, attributed by name and context.

## Price Ladder

Never publish an allocation without workings. Work down this ladder per cost line and stop at the first rung you can source; label the line with the rung you reached.

1. Observed: exact dates, two travelers, airline-direct or booking-provider rendered total.
2. Observed aggregator: exact dates and travelers on Skyscanner, then Google Flights/Kayak as the existing source order allows.
3. Derived: a rendered fare calendar, price graph or comparable-date quote scaled to the target dates with the adjustment shown. A cheapest-of-month calendar is a floor, not a quote; say so.
4. Lodging: the property's own rendered rates for the nearest available dates, plus a holiday uplift measured from that same property's other rendered dates. Never invent a multiplier.
5. Everything else: named restaurant menus, official fare tables and operator prices multiplied by counts from the itinerary.
6. No source: mark the line `unsupported budget scenario`, exclude it from the value ranking, and add a user action that names the exact page and the three-minute check that would resolve it.

If a source blocks you twice, move on and write down what the user can check in minutes. The memo reports a range and the reason for the range, for example "plan on USD 5,300 to 6,200; airfare is the swing factor", never a single unexplained total. Value is experience per dollar against a named alternative using both cost cases (`local/decision-profile.json` scoring).

## Mandatory Publication Handoff

Before the task is complete, run `npm run portal` followed by `npm run pages:sync`. The sync rebuilds the public allowlist and commits/pushes only validated `docs/`. Do not push private drafts, captures or reports. If the host runtime lacks SSH or push fails, treat publication as incomplete and report the exact error.

After validation, attempt genuine reviewer dispatch using the preference chain (ao-haiku-5-5-sc, then ao-sonnet-5-5-sc, then Qwen/Qwen-latest (ehl)) and separate host-supported contexts for each pass, highest-available first. `TRAVEL_REVIEWER_MODEL` may force one chain member. Do not skip the attempt based only on null reviewer metadata or unregistered dedicated tasks. Record concrete dispatch evidence, the actual model used, and `fallbackReason` whenever the primary is skipped; if the host cannot dispatch, retain the draft awaiting review and report the exact limitation. Never fabricate receipts or approvals.

## Price Research Before Estimates

An estimate is a fallback, not a substitute for searching. A failed deep link or generic search form is not a completed fare search. Use the rendered controls to select airports, round trip, exact dates, two travelers and currency; read them back before submitting. Wrong-date or one-traveler results are reference evidence only, never the requested offer.

For flights, use Skyscanner first for each exact-date route/window search. If Skyscanner cannot produce usable results because of a source-specific blocker, unavailable search controls, or no matching results, record the actual attempt and saved rendered evidence, then use Google Flights for that same search. Do not skip Skyscanner based on assumptions or previous runs, and do not use Google Flights as the automatic first source. Stop a source on authentication, CAPTCHA or bot defense without solving or bypassing it. Even when an aggregator succeeds, recheck the selected itinerary on at least two plausible airlines' public booking forms; an aggregator lead does not establish an airline-direct inclusive total. Compare the configured origin airports and sensible destination airports, adjacent authorized dates, baggage-inclusive economy and total travel time. Do not use self-transfers or unauthorized dates/budget increases as savings. Record each attempted route/window, source order, fallback reason, and why alternatives lost or could not be checked.

For lodging, set exact check-in/out dates, two adults and room count on a booking provider; check at least two realistic properties in suitable neighborhoods and their direct public booking pages. Compare the full stay, taxes/fees, room type, location, cancellation deadline and payment terms. A generic nightly listing is not a holiday-stay quote. Try another public provider if the first fails.

For remaining costs, replace lump-sum allocations with an itinerary-linked basket. Meals need public dated menus or defensible menu references, counts of meals and travelers, included hotel meals, currency conversion and service/tax assumptions. Transport needs the actual airport transfer and daily route/mode basket, party versus per-person fares and operator sources. Activities need the selected admission/tour, exact service date when available, traveler count, taxes, inclusions and cancellation terms from official venues/operators. Separate an insurance reference from a justified contingency reserve; do not present a reserve as an observed premium. If a source only supports reference pricing, label it as such and explain the range. Save exact URLs, observation times and rendered evidence for each basis. No source means an explicit unsupported scenario, not a precise market estimate or a value rank.

Save an effort ledger as you work: source URL, observation time, rendered search context, result/blocker and run-local capture. Describe what was actually attempted, never what should have been attempted. Document credible source-specific blockers before falling back. Do not claim best effort after only one unsuccessful URL.

For estimate candidates, include `pricingEffort: { attempts: [{ kind: "flights" | "hotels", channel: "aggregator" | "direct", source, observedAt, searchContext, result, evidence }], basis: { flights: { calculation, assumptions, evidence: [] }, hotels: { calculation, assumptions, evidence: [] } } }`. All evidence paths must also occur in `browser.evidenceFiles`, so the existing evidence hash binds them. New checkpoints require both aggregator and direct attempts for each category and evidence-linked arithmetic. This is a structural gate, not proof of external truth; reviewers assess genuine effort, date/traveler correctness and arithmetic.

Explain the actual reference dates, per-person versus party conversion, nights/rooms, fees, and any assumed seasonal adjustment. Give a justified range and sensitivity rather than an unexplained precise total. Never invent a holiday multiplier. If no defensible baseline exists, explicitly call the numeric contract value an unsupported budget scenario, not a market estimate, and do not rank value from it. Publish the uncertainty rather than fabricate an offer.

## Decision Report Quality Contract

A dossier is a decision report, not a short destination summary. New and revised checkpoints enforce `validateDossierQuality` in addition to the existing pricing-effort gate. Historical reports remain readable but do not retrospectively pass this quality gate. Validation checks structure and arithmetic, not external truth; independent reviewers still assess the research.

- Each candidate owns `ratings.value: { label, rationale, comparison, changeCondition }` and `ratings.priceConfidence: { label, rationale }` in the draft, not only an optional presentation sidecar. Value labels are `Strong fit`, `Conditional`, `Weak fit`, `Not recommended`, or `Not assessable`. Price-confidence labels are `Observed snapshot`, `Partly observed`, or `Estimate only`. Explain routing, experience fit, inclusive costs, fatigue and cancellation exposure relative to a named researched alternative; say exactly what evidence would change the label. These are reasoned qualitative judgments, not measured probabilities or reviewer approval. An unsupported budget scenario must use `Not assessable`, never a fabricated value ranking.
- Every cost line has `costBasis[key]: { basis, calculation, assumptions, quantity, unitUsd, adjustmentsUsd, totalUsd, sources, evidence }`. Arithmetic must reconcile `quantity * unitUsd + adjustmentsUsd = totalUsd = costs[key]` to one cent. Show actual reference dates, party versus per-person amounts, rooms/nights, baggage, taxes, included meals, fees and exchange-rate assumptions in the calculation. Basis is `observed`, `partly observed`, `derived`, `estimate`, or `unsupported budget scenario`. Sources must be exact public pages; evidence must be in the hashed browser evidence manifest.
- Every non-observed line also requires `rangeUsd: { low, high }` and `sensitivity`. Justify range endpoints from evidence or explicitly labeled spending scenarios; explain the changed quantity, fee, exchange rate or assumption. Do not invent seasonal multipliers. If no defensible market baseline exists, say `unsupported budget scenario` and do not derive comparative value from that number.
- Every analysis section has `paragraphs: [{ text, sources, evidence }]`, with URLs beside the claims they support. The section's existing `text` remains a readable lead, not a duplicated copy of its paragraphs. Develop the reasoning: what is known, why it matters here, the tradeoff, and uncertainty. Do not pad with generic advice or copy source prose. All paragraph evidence must also occur in `browser.evidenceFiles`.
- Every itinerary day has `visits: [{ name, significance, whyVisit, duration, logistics, cost, sources, evidence }]`. Explain what the place is known for, its historical/cultural/natural importance, why it earns time on this specific itinerary, and who might skip it. Verify seasonality and holiday hours; give sequencing, reservations, transport and a defensible cost basis or explicit unknown. Travel-only days use `visits: []` and a meaningful `travelDayReason`. A list of attraction names is not an itinerary.
- Keep at least a preferred and a meaningfully different sourced alternative when evidence permits. Explain different routes/windows, hotels/neighborhoods, inclusive costs, experience gains and losers. If an alternative could not be checked, document the actual attempt and reason rather than inventing a comparison.
- `dossier.actions` contains `{ priority: "High" | "Medium" | "Low", check, why, how, decisionImpact }`. Each action names the exact verification source/control and says what a positive or negative result changes. Keep the existing `nextChecks` list for compatibility.

Exact HTTPS provider URLs are shown next to cost derivations, attempts and claims. Public projection retains allowlisted search parameters such as property ID, travel dates, guest counts and currency, while stripping identity, account, authentication and tracking parameters. Raw browser captures remain private. A public link does not guarantee reproducibility or today's availability; always show observation time and search context.
