Advisor memo

Peru is a conditional yes at about USD 6,862 for two on the cheap fare, but about USD 8,670 or more with bags.

My recommendation

I would call Peru a conditional yes. A two-base trip, Lima for food and Cusco with one night at Machu Picchu, costs about USD 6,862 for the two of you on the lowest airfare I could read, which is only about USD 138 under your firm USD 7,000 ceiling. That number rests on an agency fare whose baggage terms are unknown and excludes checked bags, so read it as a best case, not a quote. If the fare that is actually bookable is the airline-listed one, the same trip is about USD 8,670 to 9,170, and I would drop it. Entry is simple if both US visa stamps are valid at least six months on arrival (a Peruvian consulate page says Indian passport holders then need no Peruvian visa), so there is no application lead time on that route, but the expiry on each stamp, which I have not seen, decides it. The one thing that would flip my answer is the airfare: if you can find an exact-date fare with a checked bag each at or under about USD 1,414 per person (USD 2,828 for two), the preferred build stays under your ceiling; if the best bag-inclusive fare is USD 2,200 or more per person, choose another destination.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Food and culture carry the most weight in your preferences, and this is where Peru is strongest. Lima eats at every level: the food guide I read says menu del dia lunches run S/12-20 and huariques S/25-45, while tasting menus at Central, Maido and Kjolle are S/700-1,295 per person. Cusco and the Sacred Valley add Inca stone, markets and a December that is green rather than dry. Machu Picchu is the one sight on the list that you cannot get anywhere else.

What it trades away is just as plain. There is no beach and no winter scenery, the highlands are in their rainy season, and Cusco sits at about 3,400 m. Ease of travel is middling: the flights are long, though both directions are under your 24-hour cap (11h50 out, 13h52 back).

How the trip unfolds

Lima, Dec 24-27. You land at 8:10 AM on Christmas Eve, so I keep the first day flat: a coffee and a clifftop walk in Miraflores, then Huaca Pucllana, an adobe pyramid in the middle of the district that costs S/15 each and shows that Lima is older than its Spanish centre. I would stay at Hotel Antigua Miraflores, a 9.3 from 2,144 guests at USD 352 for three nights with breakfast and free cancellation; it is not listed on the public Chase pages I read, so check the portal yourself. Christmas Day is a national holiday, so I plan plazas, the Historic Centre (UNESCO since 1988) and a ceviche lunch rather than interiors; ceviche is a lunch dish and UNESCO recognises it as part of Peru's cuisine. Dec 26 is the food day: a menu del dia lunch and dinner in Barranco, at Kjolle if you can book it. Maido's own site says it opens bookings two months ahead on the first of each month and is closed Sundays, so a December table is unlikely.

Cusco, Dec 27-Jan 4. The 8:00 AM Sky Airline flight lands at 9:25 AM, which gives a full light day at altitude. I would stay at Hostal Corihuasi (9.1 from 1,363 guests, USD 593 for the eight-night block, breakfast and free cancellation) and keep the room through the Aguas Calientes night so the big bag can stay behind; that means one night, about USD 74, is paid and unused. The Tourist Ticket (S/130 each, 10 days) covers the ruins above the city on Dec 28, then Chinchero, Moray and the Maras salt ponds by private taxi on Dec 29: Moray's terraced circles can differ by as much as 15 C from top to bottom, and the salt ponds have been worked since Inca times. LorenziGo, who has been to Cusco in December several times, says the rhythm is bright mornings and heavy afternoons, so I start every outdoor day before 8 AM.

Machu Picchu, Dec 30-31. I would take a taxi to Ollantaytambo on the 30th, see the ruins there (it was Pachacuti's royal estate), then take the train to Aguas Calientes and sleep at Casa Andina Standard (8.8, USD 145). On Dec 31 take the first buses up for an early entry. Tickets are sold on the Ministry of Culture platform; PeruRail says direct purchase started in September 2026 and recommends buying before you travel, and Dec 30-31 are capped at 5,600 visitors. I would then be back in Cusco for New Year's Eve; kimkim says the late-December weeks pull in Peruvian families and tighten bookings, so reserve dinner early.

New Year to departure. Jan 1 is a holiday and a rest day with Tourist Ticket museums if they are open. Jan 2 is Pisac, Jan 3 is a flex day for the South Valley or the Cathedral, and Jan 4 is the 7:55 PM flight to Lima and a night at Wyndham Costa del Sol (USD 125) before the 12:05 PM flight home.

What it will really cost

For two travellers on the preferred build: flights USD 3,301 (the agency fare for the international leg, USD 2,697, plus USD 604 on Sky Airline to Cusco and back), thirteen paid nights (twelve distinct beds) USD 1,215, meals about USD 927, ground transport about USD 790 (mostly the train at about USD 300 and taxis), activities about USD 188 (the Machu Picchu entry is S/163 each) and USD 441 for insurance and a 5 percent reserve. That adds to USD 6,862. The total is a lower bound for the Maras salt ponds, Cusco Cathedral and San Blas, whose entry fees I could not find (allow about S/60 each as an assumption). The ranges matter more than the point: every line at its low end is about USD 4,630 (using a 20h51 outbound that skips Christmas Eve), every line at its high end about USD 10,820.

Which numbers are real: the hotel totals and the Skyscanner and United results are exact-date snapshots from today. Meals, taxis, the train and activities are derived from guides and Numbeo, not from dated booking pages. The one number I distrust is the USD 1,349 flight. The same itinerary listed by LATAM on Skyscanner is USD 2,448 each with bags, United's own page starts at USD 2,210 each, and the LATAM and Alaska direct pages failed on me, so I could not prove the cheap fare is real or what bags it carries. On the lean build at the United fare, with the cheapest decent beds, the total is still USD 7,581.

Value: against Portugal (conditional at about USD 6,977, taken from the earlier Portugal dossier) this costs about the same and gives you Machu Picchu and a better food scene, but it also gives you altitude, rain and a longer flight.

Where this can go wrong

The weather is the most predictable risk. In Cusco's WMO normals December has 114.9 mm of rain and January 145.8 mm, against 5.2 mm in August, and Exploor describes Machu Picchu as often foggy in December. Altitude is the second risk: Cusco is at 3,400 m and the Sacred Valley plateau at 3,300-3,500 m, so the first days are light and you should talk to a clinician about altitude before leaving. The third is holiday friction: Dec 25 and Jan 1 are national holidays and I could not confirm any museum or church hours for them. The fourth is documents: if either US visa stamp expires before about June 24, 2027 you do not qualify for the visa-free route. Who should not choose this trip: anyone who wants a beach, snow, or a short flight.

The best alternative

The strongest competitor is Portugal: about the same cost, one base, a nonstop out and none of the altitude. It loses on Machu Picchu and on how deep the food scene goes. I would switch to Portugal if the only bookable Peru fare is USD 2,200 or more per person, or if either of your US visas fails the six-month test.

What to check next

  • Read the expiry date on both US visa stamps; it must be about June 24, 2027 or later. If either is earlier, the visa-free route is gone and the trip needs a consular visa.
  • Price SFO-LIM, Dec 23-Jan 5, two adults, on the airline websites (LATAM, Delta, Alaska), with the bags included. If the international total with bags is at or under about USD 2,828 for two the preferred build stays under the ceiling; at USD 4,420 (the United floor) it is about USD 8,670.
  • Open tuboleto.cultura.pe and check which Machu Picchu routes and morning slots exist for Dec 31.
  • Look at the PeruRail and IncaRail Ollantaytambo to Aguas Calientes schedule and fare for Dec 30 and Dec 31 and confirm that a train arrives at least 90 minutes before your entry hour.
  • Ask the hotels to confirm Dec 25 and Jan 1 hours for the museums and restaurants you want, and book dinner on Dec 24 and Dec 31.

  7. Day 7 · 2026-12-29 · Cusco: Chinchero, Moray and Maras

    Hire a private taxi for the day at about 8 AM: Chinchero first for its town and weaving, then Moray's terraced circular depressions on the high plateau, then the Maras salt ponds, back in Cusco by 5 PM. This is the high plateau at 3,300-3,500 m, so go slowly, carry water and a rain shell and be off the plateau before the afternoon storms.

    Chinchero

    Why it matters: Chinchero is the capital of its district in the Urubamba province, a mainly Quechua community and the proposed site of a Cusco airport (Wikipedia); it is on the Tourist Ticket.

    Why this visit: It is the first stop on the plateau and a place where weaving is still a living craft.

    Time: 1-1.5 hours. Covered by Tourist Ticket Circuit III (S/70 partial, or the full ticket).

    Cost basis: Included in the full Tourist Ticket.

    Moray

    Why it matters: An Inca site about 50 km north-west of Cusco at about 3,500 m with terraced circular depressions up to 30 m deep, producing a temperature difference of as much as 15 C from top to bottom (Wikipedia).

    Why this visit: It is the most unusual Inca site near Cusco and the farm-lab explanation is a good story.

    Time: 1 hour. On the Tourist Ticket; paved route via Maras.

    Cost basis: Included in the full Tourist Ticket.
