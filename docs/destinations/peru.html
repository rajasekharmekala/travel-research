Advisor memo

Peru is a conditional yes: Lima, Cusco and Machu Picchu for about $6,541 for two

My recommendation

I would call Peru a conditional yes, built as two bases: four nights in Lima for the food, then Cusco and the Sacred Valley for the culture and the scenery, with one night in Aguas Calientes so Machu Picchu gets a morning of its own. Priced from public pages it comes to about $6,541 for the two of you, with a realistic range of about $5,348 to $9,372. The middle case sits about $459 under your firm USD 7,000 ceiling, so there is room for one splurge dinner but not for the faster flights home.

Entry is the easy part and the one thing to check carefully. Indian passport holders are exempt from a Peruvian tourist visa when they hold a US visa that is valid for at least six months on the day they arrive. You told me your US stamps cover the return date, which is January 5. That is not the same as covering June 2027, so the single thing that would flip this trip is a stamp that expires before about June 23, 2027.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Food and culture is where Peru is strongest, and I would not trade it for anything else on your list. Lima is a city where a S/12 market lunch and a S/750 tasting menu share a week, and the Andes give you Inca stonework that is still the working architecture of Cusco. Nature is a clear second: Machu Picchu sits on a cloud-forest ridge at about 2,430 m, and the Sacred Valley is green in December.

It trades away the beach and any real winter scenery. Lima is sunny and in its summer in December, but the Pacific is not a swimming proposition for most people, and the Andes are in their rainy season, not under snow. I scored it about 60 out of 100 against your weights (food and culture 5, beach 1, winter scenery 2, nature 4, ease 2). Ease is the low mark: this is a 12-hour trip out, a 22-hour trip home on the cheaper fare, a domestic hop, and 3,400 m of altitude in Cusco.

How the trip unfolds

Lima, December 23 to 27. You land at 9:50 PM on the 23rd after a 6:30 AM departure through Los Angeles, so the first night is only a taxi and a bed. I would stay at Hotel Limade in Miraflores, which showed a 9.1 guest score from 1,090 reviews, breakfast and free cancellation at about $77 a night on the dates I checked. Spend Christmas Eve at Huaca Pucllana, a 1,500-year-old adobe pyramid in the middle of Miraflores that is a better first look at Lima than any museum, then eat dinner early. The Peru Spirit food guide makes the point I would repeat: you do not need Central or Maido to eat brilliantly in Lima. Market stalls at Surquillo serve cooked lunches for S/12 to S/18, and the guide says a generous chifa plate in the Barrio Chino runs S/25 to S/45 and feeds two. I would still book one tasting menu, and Maido is the one I would pick, at about S/750 to S/880 a head. Christmas Day is the unknown: I could not confirm which Lima sites open, so walk the Plaza Mayor area and the coast instead of planning around a ticketed visit.

Cusco, December 27 to 30. The 6:00 AM LATAM flight lands at 7:25 AM, which gives you a full first day at 3,400 m. Do almost nothing. Gabriel Lorenzi of LorenziGo describes the Cusco December rhythm well: a bright, cloudless morning, a wall of gray cloud around 3:30 PM, then a proper downpour. Plan the outdoor sites for the morning and keep the afternoons for churches, markets and food. I would stay at Hostal Corihuasi, a 9.1 from 1,364 reviews at about $74 a night with breakfast, 0.2 miles from downtown. Sacsayhuamán, the Inca fortress above the city at 3,701 m, is the day-two payoff, and a Boleto Turístico (S/130 each, about $35) covers it along with Pisac, Moray and Ollantaytambo. The day after, take a driver to Pisac in the morning and be back before the rain.

Aguas Calientes and Machu Picchu, December 30 to 31. Take a taxi to Ollantaytambo, walk its terraces, and catch an afternoon train to Aguas Calientes. I priced the standard tourist class at about $150 a head round trip, from the Peru Spirit rail guide that puts it at $130 to $170. One night at Casa Andina Standard Machu Picchu (8.9 from 618 reviews, about $145 with breakfast and taxes) puts you at the bus queue early. The Exploor Peru guide recommends a 9 AM to 2 PM entry window because mornings are usually drier, and it warns that trail and road closures are possible in the wet season. Highland Adventures on MachuPicchu.com is blunt that the Christmas and New Year weeks behave like peak season inside an otherwise quiet month, so you should treat the entry ticket and the train as the scarce pieces. Since September 2026 the entry ticket is bought directly on the Ministry of Culture portal.

Cusco again, December 31 to January 4. Be back in Cusco for New Year's Eve. Kimkim notes that Cusco's New Year is famous across Peru, with fireworks over the plaza, and that crowds spike in the last ten days of the month. Rest on New Year's Day, spend January 2 at Moray, keep January 3 free as a weather day, and fly home to Lima on the 7:55 PM Sky Airline flight on the 4th. The Inca Net Adventure guide adds that the Santurantikuy artisan market runs on December 24, so you will miss it while in Lima. That is a fair loss.

Lima, January 4 to 5. You land at 9:35 PM, sleep in Miraflores, and use January 5 for the one thing the first days did not cover: a lunch-only ceviche, since the guide notes that ceviche is traditionally a noon to 4 PM meal, and a walk along the Miraflores cliffs. The flight leaves at 11:40 PM and lands in San Francisco at 6:47 PM on January 6.

What it will really cost

Flights are the biggest number and the most solid one. Skyscanner showed LATAM, operated by Delta and LATAM Peru, at $1,172 per person ($2,344 for two), with no nonstop from San Francisco to Lima. The cheapest fare with both directions inside your 24-hour cap has a 22h07 return through Atlanta. Add $238 per person for the domestic LATAM and Sky Airline legs ($476) and flights come to $2,820. These are aggregator figures. LATAM's own booking page would not render fares for me, so no airline-direct total exists yet.

Hotels are $1,049 across thirteen nights: five in Lima at about $77, seven in Cusco at about $74 and one in Aguas Calientes at $145. Those are observed Booking.com snapshots, but for dates one day off from the plan, and I did not measure a New Year uplift. Food is $1,190: thirteen days at $60 for two plus a tasting-menu dinner at about $440 that replaces a $30 one. Transport is $744, mostly the train ($300) plus airport and day-trip taxis. Activities are $288, led by the Machu Picchu entry at about $65 each through a reseller that adds 5 percent, plus a guide at $40 each. A reserve of $450 covers bags and surprises, and it is a reserve, not an insurance quote. Add it up and you get $6,541.

On value, 60 experience points per $1,000 gives about 9.2 in the middle case, about 11.2 at the low end of $5,348 and about 6.4 at the high end of $9,372. The big swing is meals and hotels around New Year, then airfare. The faster return (13h52 via Atlanta, $3,285 for the long-haul fare) adds $941 and lands the middle case at $7,482, over your ceiling.

Where this can go wrong

Rain is the main risk. This is the wet season in the Andes, with about 80 to 100 mm of rain in December according to LorenziGo, and afternoon downpours are normal. Machu Picchu is open on Christmas and New Year's Day, but wet-season road and trail closures can happen. Altitude is the second risk: Cusco is at about 3,400 m and the first day should be a rest day. The 22-hour return is the third. It is inside your cap by two hours and it is a long, tiring way to finish two weeks. If that bothers you, the $941 upgrade is the honest price of fixing it.

Who should not choose this: anyone who wants a beach, snow, or a relaxed one-base trip. It is a hard-working holiday with five hotel changes.

The best alternative

The strongest competing itinerary is the same route on the faster return, at about $7,482, which fails your ceiling. Beyond that, I did not price a one-base option, because Cusco alone is a weak food trip and Lima alone has no Andes. If the Cusco half is dropped, the honest competitor is a different country. The Portugal dossier is cooler, has a TAP nonstop and carries a Schengen visa you must obtain in advance at about $7,010; Peru has no visa step and costs less in the middle case, but it asks for more flights and more physical effort.

What to check next

  • US visa validity. Look at the expiry date on both stamps. If either ends before about June 23, 2027, I would stop and read the Peruvian consular visa route before anything else.
  • LATAM direct total. Open latamairlines.com, set San Francisco to Lima, round trip, December 23 to January 5, two adults. A direct total above about $3,100 for the long-haul fare moves this to a weak fit.
  • Machu Picchu entry. Check the Ministry of Culture portal for a December 31 slot in the morning window and the Ollantaytambo to Aguas Calientes train times on peru rail and Inca Rail.
  • Maido reservations. Check whether a table exists on a December 25 to 26 evening. If not, the tasting-menu uplift of $410 disappears and the total drops to about $6,131.
  • Chase Travel. None of the four hotels was checked on a public Chase page; look in the portal yourselves.

Your Action Plan

Resolve high-priority checks before nonrefundable commitments. Verified routing, inclusive prices and eligibility can change the value assessment; changed hours or weather can change the itinerary.

  1. High priority: Check the expiry date on both travelers' US visa stamps for at least six months of validity on December 23 (about June 23, 2027).

    Why: The Peruvian exemption for Indian passport holders depends on the US visa being valid six months from arrival; the user-attested validity runs only through the return date.

    How to check: Read the expiry date printed on each US visa; then read the Indian-citizen row on the official consulado.pe requirements document.

    Decision impact: If both stamps run past about June 23, 2027, entry is a non-issue; if either does not, the trip needs a consular visa and the plan stops until that is solved.
