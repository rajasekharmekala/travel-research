Advisor memo

Spain misses the USD 7,000 ceiling by about USD 1,000 on its best build, so treat it as conditional on finding savings and a visa appointment.

My recommendation

I would not book Spain for this window as it stands. I would keep it as a conditional option and only if you can find about USD 1,000 of savings. The best version I can build is twelve nights split three in Madrid, three in Seville and six back in Madrid, with Toledo and Segovia as day trips once the Christmas shutdown passes. At the observed flight fare and observed nightly hotel rates it comes to USD 7,987 for the two of you, USD 987 over your firm USD 7,000 ceiling. My range for it is USD 6,466 to USD 10,350, and the only way under the ceiling is for nearly every flexible line to land at its floor.

Entry is a process you can finish but not a formality. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa, and for residents of the Bay Area the Spanish consulate in San Francisco handles it through BLS, in person, by appointment, at least 30 days before travel. That makes 2026-11-23 the last safe application date, about six weeks from today. The one thing that would flip my view is the airfare: at about USD 3,647 it is nearly half the budget, and I only saw it on an aggregator.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Food and culture is where Spain is strongest. Madrid gives you the Prado, the Royal Palace and the tapas lanes around Plaza Mayor, and Seville gives you the Alcázar, the Cathedral and the Santa Cruz alleys. It is also the warmer base: the 30-year December average high is 16 °C in Seville against 10 °C in Madrid, which is the winter-scenery argument for going south, though Seville is wetter at about 76 mm against 44 mm.

What it trades away is the beach, entirely. Nothing in this route touches the coast, and I would not sell it as a nature trip either, although Retiro park is a UNESCO-listed green space in the middle of Madrid. Ease of travel is mixed: about 19 hours each way with two stops, then a very walkable trip once you land.

How the trip unfolds

You land in Madrid at 3:20 PM on Christmas Eve after the Delta and KLM routing through Seattle and Paris. I would put you in the Novotel Madrid City Las Ventas for the first three nights: guests rate it 4.5 across about 5,900 reviews and it was the best-value well-reviewed hotel I saw at USD 131. I have not checked how far it is from Sol, so treat it as a metro or taxi ride from the old centre. Christmas Day is the dead day. A Rick Steves forum regular who spent the holiday in Seville said everything was open and bustling except for a shutdown that starts on the afternoons of the 24th and 31st, and that on the 25th and 1st only a couple of restaurants tied to big hotels were serving. So book a Christmas lunch early and spend the rest of the day in Retiro, which does not close.

On Saturday the 26th the Prado reopens and earns a full morning. It holds the Spanish royal collection, with Velázquez, Goya and El Greco among its best-represented artists, and I would pick three or four rooms and skip the rest. The Royal Palace takes Sunday morning, the 27th, and then the afternoon train to Seville. Renfe had not posted the holiday timetable when a forum poster asked, so keep that day flexible.

Seville is three nights at the Hotel Alcántara, rated 4.5 by about 760 guests at USD 145, in the Santa Cruz area. If you want one special night, the Hotel Murillo Reinoso scores 4.8, but from only 130 reviews. Spend the 28th on the Alcázar and the Cathedral, both part of the same UNESCO World Heritage listing, and the 29th on Plaza de España, built for the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition, and an evening in Triana. Take the train back on the 30th.

The last six nights are Madrid again. New Year's Eve is the main event: the grapes at midnight under the clock tower in Puerta del Sol, with an early dinner booked well ahead because restaurants run set menus and many close early. January 1 is closed almost everywhere, so I would walk the old centre and not plan a single ticketed thing. Toledo and Segovia fill the 2nd and 3rd; Toledo for its walled historic centre, Segovia for the Roman aqueduct. The 4th is a slow day, because your flight leaves at 6:05 AM on the 5th and you will need a taxi around 3 AM.

What it will really cost

Here is the arithmetic for the build I recommend. Flights are USD 3,647 for two, a Skyscanner snapshot of the Delta and KLM itinerary from San Jose. Hotels are USD 1,614: nine Madrid nights at USD 131 and three Seville nights at USD 145, from Google Hotels. Meals are an estimate of USD 1,624, about EUR 100 a day for two from Numbeo price levels at 1.12 dollars per euro, plus a holiday set-menu premium. Ground transport is USD 400, activities USD 300, and the last line is USD 402: USD 252 in consulate and BLS fees and USD 150 for insurance and a reserve.

The central version, with Hotel Europa by Puerta del Sol in place of the Novotel, is USD 8,440. Madrid only, central, is USD 8,413. So Seville costs almost nothing extra and I would keep it.

Observed means seen on a public page on one day, October 11. Flights and nightly hotel rates are observed on aggregators only; everything else is an estimate with a stated range. On value, USD 7,987 does not clear the ceiling, and I would not rank Spain's value higher than a destination that fits.

Where this can go wrong

Closures are the biggest risk. Christmas Day, New Year's Day and the afternoons before them shut most sights and many restaurants. One Spain booking guide notes that holiday apartments often require three to seven night minimums across those dates, which is why I priced hotels. The Three Kings parade on January 5 is the evening of your departure, so you miss it.

Fatigue is real: about 19 hours out and 19 back with two stops each way, and a 6:05 AM return that begins in the middle of the night. The visa needs an in-person appointment and I could not see availability. Who should skip this trip: anyone who wants beach time, or who cannot tolerate a dead Christmas Day.

The best alternative

The strongest alternative inside Spain is Madrid alone with central hotels and the two day trips, at USD 8,413. It avoids two train rides and a hotel change, but loses Seville, which is the reason to go in winter, and it costs USD 426 more than my build. I would switch to it only if the Dec 27 or Dec 30 train times turn out poor.

What to check next

First, confirm both of you hold US visa stamps the consulate accepts; it says B-1/B-2 holders cannot apply in the United States. Second, open the BLS San Francisco booking page and see whether appointments exist before 2026-11-23; none was checked. Third, reprice the flight on Air France, KLM or Delta directly, because the United site errored and the Air France form was not completed. Fourth, decide whether you would stretch toward USD 8,000. If not, Spain drops out. Finally, check The Edit and Chase Travel yourself for these hotels, since their property lists need a login and this research never signed in.

Application steps
  1. Check that each traveler holds a Green Card or a valid US long-term visa and a passport valid for at least 90 days after leaving Schengen with two blank pages.
  2. Book an appointment through the official BLS Spain San Francisco site (the consulate says an appointment is mandatory); do not pay third parties for appointments.
  3. Prepare the signed application form, photo, proof of residence in the jurisdiction, round-trip flight reservation, hotel reservation for the full stay, three months of US bank statements, insurance certificate and cover letter.
  4. Attend the appointment in person at 717 Market St, Suite 425, San Francisco, pay the consulate fee by money order and the BLS fee by debit card.
  5. Track the application; the consulate's minimum processing time is 15 working days and BLS asks for submission at least 30 days before travel.

  • Round-trip flight reservation and hotel reservation matching the dates (do not buy tickets before the visa)
  • Three months of US bank statements and the latest savings and credit card statements
  • Schengen travel insurance certificate with at least EUR 30,000 cover and repatriation
