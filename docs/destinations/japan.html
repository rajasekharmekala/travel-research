Climate and weather

December planning uses regional climatology rather than a distant forecast. JNTO separates Kanto/Tokyo, Kansai/Kyoto and Osaka, Hokuriku/Shinetsu, Tohoku and Hokkaido; those regions have materially different winter rain, snow and daylight exposure. Tokyo–Kyoto/Osaka is primarily a cool city itinerary with possible clear winter views, not a guaranteed snow trip. Outdoor plans should be flexible and a short-range forecast recheck is needed before travel; northern or Sea-of-Japan scenery is a stronger snow alternative but adds rail and weather risk.

Experiences and closures

The preferred experience spine is Tokyo neighborhoods and food, followed by Kyoto temples/gardens and an Osaka food-focused finish or reverse order depending on the final flight. New Year is a real operational compromise: the public planning source describes congestion around Dec 29–31 and Jan 2–4, and closures from Dec 29–Jan 4, especially Jan 1. Front-load museums and timed attractions, reserve high-demand restaurants, and preserve shrines/temples, neighborhood walks, department-store openings and indoor food experiences for closure-sensitive days. Verify every official 2026/2027 calendar rather than assuming a nationwide schedule.

Alternatives and pushback

The strongest competing itinerary is Tokyo-only for all 10 nights: fewer lodging moves, better closure/weather resilience and potentially lower transport cost, but less Kyoto/Osaka culture. A KIX open-jaw can reduce backtracking only if a single-ticket fare is competitive. A Dec 19 departure or Jan 6 return gives more pace but is remote-work dependent and adds lodging/meal costs; those windows cannot be treated as free flexibility. Pushback: Japan is not automatically a value destination during New Year, and snow scenery is not guaranteed in Tokyo/Kyoto. Travelers who dislike crowds, cool weather, long flights or closure-driven planning may prefer a warmer beach destination; if documents or the ceiling fail, this candidate should be rejected rather than rationalized.
