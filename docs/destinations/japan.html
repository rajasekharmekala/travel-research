Advisor memo

Japan is a great trip but only the 1-stop fare fits your USD 7,000 ceiling, and only just

My recommendation

I would not book Japan for the two of you unless you accept a modest margin, because the trip is wonderful but the airfare in New Year week eats the budget. Tokyo, Kyoto and Tokyo again, on a 1-stop fare through Los Angeles, comes to about USD 7,223 for two on my numbers, with a range from USD 6,670 to USD 9,248. The nonstop Japan Airlines version I would prefer to fly is USD 8,276 (range USD 7,724 to 9,798), which is over your firm USD 7,000 ceiling even in its best case.

The entry visa is a process you can finish: Indian passport holders need a Japanese visa, and with valid US visas and a Bay Area address you can apply online with the San Francisco consulate. The published minimum is 5 business days after a complete application, there is no expedite, and my last safe date for a Dec 23 departure is Nov 23, 2026 (30 days out, because MOFA's processing page (dated December 5, 2022) warns problem applications can take more than a month; the bare minimum would be about Dec 11). The online route is only open to travelers with a valid US visa other than B1/B2 or a green card, so check each traveler's visa category first; I have not seen it recorded.

The one result that flips this: an airline-direct 1-stop fare with checked bags at or under about USD 4,450 keeps the low case under your ceiling. Anything much higher and I would take Japan off your list.

Why this trip fits the two of you

It serves your food and culture preference better than any trip I can think of: Tokyo and Kyoto are two of the best food cities in the world, and temples and shrines are free and open every day of the New Year holiday. Nature gets one proper day, in Hakone, with a real chance at Mount Fuji in December air that is dry and clear. Winter scenery is a partial trade: Tokyo and Kyoto are cold and sunny (highs of 9-12 C) and almost never snowy, so you get illuminations and temple gardens, not snow. There is no beach. Ease of travel is excellent once you land, because it is all rail and every day trip leaves from a station.

What it trades away is money and time: about 20 hours each way on the cheap routing, and a week in which many independent restaurants and museums shut. I have built the plan around what stays open.

How the trip unfolds

Dec 23-25: the flight. You leave SFO at 4:35 PM on Dec 23 and land at Haneda at 5:30 AM on Dec 25, because the route crosses the date line. I would start the hotel on Dec 25 and ask about early check-in and luggage storage (the Dec 24 night would cost about USD 104 and guarantee a room), then go straight to Tsukiji Outer Market for breakfast and finish with a gentle first afternoon at Senso-ji in Asakusa, Tokyo's oldest temple, which is free and lit in the evening. I would book Comfort Hotel Tokyo Higashi Nihombashi (about USD 311 for three nights on the 1-stop plan, USD 415 for four on the nonstop; breakfast included, rated 8.4): it is a few minutes from Tokyo Station for the Shinkansen and on the subway line that runs straight to Haneda. I could not confirm whether it is a Chase Travel property because that list needs your login.

Dec 26-27: the two big Tokyo days. I would put Meiji Jingu on Dec 26 morning, a forest shrine beside Harajuku that draws about 3.1 million people over New Year, and Shibuya Sky at sunset (JPY 2,700 each online). Dec 27 is Hakone: the Odakyu train from Shinjuku with a Freepass (JPY 7,100 each), the ropeway over the Owakudani steam vents and the Lake Ashi boat. If Fuji is hidden by fog, you still have a beautiful loop; if it is clear it is the best scenery of the trip.

Dec 28-Jan 3: Kyoto. Take the Nozomi from Tokyo on Dec 28 (about 2h15, JPY 14,170 each) and go straight to Kiyomizu-dera for dusk. I would stay near Kyoto Station at Comfort Hotel ERA Kyoto Horikawagojo (USD 637 for six nights, 8.8), because Fushimi Inari is 5 minutes away, and Nara, Osaka and Arashiyama are each one train from the same station. For New Year, the central Hotel Gracery Kyoto Sanjo (USD 1,072) is better if the ceiling ever loosens, because Kawaramachi and Kiyamachi are where food stays open on Jan 1.

Komachi, who spent New Year in Kyoto and wrote about it on note.com, found Nishiki Market packed but open on Dec 30, shops closing before 6 PM on New Year's Eve, and almost nothing independent open on Jan 1 away from Kawaramachi. That is why I eat early on Dec 31, and why Dec 31 evening is Okera Mairi at Yasaka Shrine in Gion instead of the Chion-in bell, which WaTabi reports is now a paid, advance-application-only event. Nijo Castle is out because the Ninomaru Palace is closed from Dec 26. Jan 1 is slow: a late start, hatsumode at Yasaka, and lunch on Kawaramachi. Jan 2 is Osaka for Osaka Castle and Dotonbori, because department stores and many shops reopen then.

Jan 3-5: Tokyo again. Japan-guide expects Jan 3 to be the peak return day for the whole country, so take the earliest reserved seat; if that fails, move to Jan 2 evening. You end in Asakusa at APA Hotel Asakusa Ekimae (USD 93 for two nights) with Senso-ji for hatsumode, the Marunouchi illumination, Skytree on Jan 4 and the Keikyu/Asakusa line direct to Haneda on Jan 5 for the 7:40 PM flight.

Pace is steady, with one big day trip every other day and nothing before 8 AM except the Nara and Arashiyama mornings. Selfguidejapan.com reminds readers that December gives dry sunny cities but that for snow you go north, which is why I did not add a mountain overnight.

What it will really cost

For two on the 1-stop plan I get USD 7,223: flights 4,123, hotels 1,081, meals 955, rail and local transport 587, activities 87 (Shibuya Sky, Skytree and three temples), and 390 for the JAPAN eVISA fee (JPY 15,000 each) plus a USD 200 reserve. The flights and hotels were rendered on exact dates for two adults; everything else is my estimate. Meals use JPY 6,300 per person per day (breakfast JPY 700, lunch JPY 1,500, dinner JPY 4,100; an Ichiran bowl in Tokyo is JPY 1,180) at 158.31 yen to the dollar, so about USD 80 a day for two.

The low case is USD 6,670 (the cheaper Kyoto hotel, konbini breakfasts, no Hakone, no Shibuya Sky or Skytree) and it assumes two checked bags per traveler are included in the fare, as United's own Pacific fares show; the high case adds up to USD 400 of bag fees. Tenryu-ji and Osaka Castle tower admissions were not captured and sit only in the high case. The high case is USD 9,248. Only the low case is under USD 7,000 and the margin is about USD 330.

The nonstop JAL plan costs USD 1,053 more. It saves 8h25 flying out and 5h45 flying back and gives you the whole of Dec 24 evening, and I think that is a better trip, but at USD 8,276 it does not fit. Value, in experience per dollar, is strong once you land (rooms at USD 47-107 a night, most sights free) and poor on the flight. If you move the departure to Dec 19, the nonstop alone is USD 6,564, so do not.

Where this can go wrong

The biggest risk is the fare itself: the Skyscanner total was not reproduced on an airline page, and a checked bag or a repricing could push you over the ceiling. Second is a missed connection in Los Angeles on a 19h55 outbound journey. Third is entry: a returned visa application with no expedite and no status checks could cost you the departure date. Fourth is the Jan 3 train: reserve early.

Weather risk is small in this season: Hakone fog, a cold rain day, perhaps a few centimeters of snow. New Year closures are real: Breeze Japan says government offices close Dec 29-Jan 3 and department stores mostly shut on Jan 1.

This is not the trip for someone who dislikes crowds, long flights, or planned days at a shrine with a million people. It is also not for a snow trip, because Tokyo and Kyoto rarely see it.

The best alternative

The strongest competing itinerary is the same route on the nonstop JAL fare at USD 8,276. If you raise the ceiling, I would switch to it without hesitation: it adds a day and removes about 14 hours of travel. I would also switch if a nonstop round trip at or under about USD 4,300 for two with bags appears on the same dates.

A Dec 19 departure is not an alternative: the exact-date search priced the nonstop at USD 6,564 and added four more hotel nights. OAK and SJC have no nonstop flights and cost more than SFO.

  • Reprice the 1-stop and nonstop fares airline-direct with checked bags, and price Narita (NRT) round trips too on united.com and ana.co.jp (and jal.co.jp in a normal browser). A 1-stop total up to about USD 4,450 keeps the low case under the ceiling; a higher total means Japan is out.
  • Decide whether the 19h55 outbound leg and the dawn arrival are acceptable. If not, the only alternative is the nonstop at USD 8,276, over the ceiling.
  • Reserve Shinkansen seats at the one-month sale (Nov 28 for Dec 28; Dec 3 for Jan 3).
  • Check the Chion-in event page in December and the Kyoto closures PDF. A ticket adds about USD 38; sold out keeps Okera Mairi.
  • Ask the Tokyo hotel about early check-in for the 5:30 AM arrival; if refused, add the Dec 24 night (about USD 104).
  • Check the four stays in the Chase Travel portal while signed in. A match is upside only and is never subtracted from cost.

  1. Confirm eligibility on the consulate page: valid US visa or green card (NOT ESTA or B1/B2), residence in the SF jurisdiction, tourism only, single entry; note each traveler's visa category.
  2. Prepare each traveler's documents from the consulate Checklist (passport, photo within 6 months, proof of US visa status, itinerary).
  3. Create an eVISA account on https://www.evisa.mofa.go.jp/ and submit one application per traveler (a proxy needs a letter of authorization).
  4. Wait for the review (at least 5 business days) and watch the status under "Visa application list" in the portal; no status inquiries are accepted.

Experiences and closures

The itinerary is built around what stays open at New Year: shrines, temples, parks, observation decks, department-store districts and chain restaurants, while museums and many independent restaurants close.

Japan-guide states that temples and shrines do not close at New Year, but many tourist attractions, shops and restaurants close on one or more days between December 29 and January 4, especially January 1; museums typically close for multiple days.     Why: Entry authorization is a process with a published minimum of 5 business days, no expedite and no status checks, and approval is a gate for every Japan booking.

    How to check: First read each traveler's US visa category (the eVISA route excludes B1/B2 and ESTA; a green card or a non-visitor visa qualifies per the consulate page).
