Advisor memo

Vietnam is a strong food-and-culture trip for about $7,104, a little over your ceiling; conditional on the airfare and the island hotel.

My recommendation

I would take Vietnam only as a conditional trip: fly SFO to Ho Chi Minh City on China Airlines via Taipei on Dec 23, spend seven nights in District 1, then three beach nights on Phu Quoc from Jan 1 and a last night back in the city. Plan on about USD 6,281 to 7,589 for the two of you: the low case is $6,281.42, the middle case is $7,103.51 ($103.51 over your firm USD 7,000 ceiling), and the middle case with China Airlines' own listed fare is $7,588.51. The extreme case with every line at the top of its range is $8,615.79.

Entry is an e-visa for each of you, USD 25 each on the Immigration Department's page. Two Vietnamese government pages disagree on processing time: 3 working days on one, and 5 to 30 on the other, which also advises filing 7 to 40 working days before departure. I would treat Oct 28, 2026, about 14 days from today, as the last safe application date for planning, and start the application before you buy anything.

The one thing that flips this: if the airfare is nearer the airline's own listed $4,881 than the cheapest provider's $4,392, or the island hotel is nearer $700 than $300, the trip is over the ceiling and I would drop Phu Quoc.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Food and culture is the preference this trip serves best, and it is the one with the most weight. Ho Chi Minh City gives you street food at a few dollars a bowl, a market, a museum that makes sense of the country, a half-day at the Cu Chi tunnels, a day in the Mekong Delta and an afternoon in Chinatown, all without changing hotels. Nature is middling: the Mekong boats and the Phu Quoc islands, not mountains. Beach is real but not guaranteed, because the Jan 1 to 4 island nights fall in the weeks when drifting water hyacinth and waste reached the southeast beaches in 2026. Winter scenery does not exist here; it is 30 to 32 C and dry in December.

Ease of travel is the weak point. The flight crosses the date line, so Dec 24 disappears and you land at 10:00 AM on Christmas Day. Each leg is under your 24 hour cap, 19h20 out and 16h30 back with one stop in Taipei, but you are in the air a long time and the jet lag is real. On value, I would rate food and culture 5 out of 5, nature 3, beach 3, ease 3 and winter scenery 1; that works out to about 66 out of 100 on your weights, which is a respectable fit rather than a great one.

How the trip unfolds

Getting there (Dec 23 to 25). CI3 leaves SFO at 11:40 PM on Dec 23, lands in Taipei at 5:45 AM on Dec 25 local time, and CI781 reaches Ho Chi Minh City at 10:00 AM. The 1h45 connection stays airside. The Taiwan Bureau of Consular Affairs says you need no visa if you stay in the international transit area, but the page is a general 2017 FAQ that does not name Indian passports, and an Auckland office page says all travelers must fill in a Taiwan online arrival card, so I would ask the airline before booking.

Ho Chi Minh City, seven nights (Dec 25 to Jan 1). I would book Adora Hotel in District 1: a 4-star about five minutes' walk from Ben Thanh Market with breakfast included and a rooftop sauna, rated 8.0 from 1,044 guests, and $572 for the seven nights on Booking.com. If you want a higher score and do not mind a room without a window, NiK Hotel Thi Sach is 9.3 from 236 guests at $776. I could not confirm Chase Travel status for either; the portal needs your own login.

Christmas Day is easy: the Post Office, the Notre-Dame square and Ben Thanh Market on foot. Joy Nguyen of Day Trips Vietnam says the city is gridlocked around Notre-Dame and Nguyen Hue on Christmas Eve and back to work on the morning of the 25th, which is one small advantage of landing on the 25th. The cathedral interior is closed for restoration, so go for the square.

Dec 26 is the history day: the War Remnants Museum when it opens at 7:30, then the Reunification Palace, which stays open on holidays. Dec 27 is Cu Chi in the morning. Dec 28 is the Mekong Delta on a group tour at about $33 each: the boat among the islands and a pagoda at My Tho. Dec 29 is Cholon, Binh Tay Market and Thien Hau Temple, a temple built around 1760 for the sea goddess Mazu. Dec 30 is the Opera House, Dong Khoi and the Bitexco Skydeck at dusk.

I would spend New Year Eve in the city on purpose. Nguyen Hue Walking Street is the free party, with a DJ stage from 19:00 and fireworks at midnight last year, though nothing is published for this year yet. Joy Nguyen also warns that resorts commonly attach compulsory $50 to $150 per person gala dinners on Dec 24 and 31, and that Dec 20 to Jan 3 runs 25 to 40 percent above normal. The Adora Hotel rate averaged about $82 a night across the seven nights, but the Phu Quoc hotels I looked at for a Dec 29 to Jan 4 stay ran $193 to $333 a night.

Phu Quoc, three nights (Jan 1 to 4). The flight is about an hour. I would put you at Phu Quoc Bambusa Resort in Ong Lang on the calmer west coast: 8.8 from 521 guests, breakfast and a pool, $674 for the three nights. I picked the west coast because Tu Nguyen of VnExpress reported Khem and Sao beaches covered with water hyacinth and waste at the start of 2026, and one visitor who stayed ten days near Khem Beach had not dared to swim. Lilia of Travel Inspire is less alarmed and says the southern beaches tend to stay cleaner, so I would look at photographs the week before. If you want the cheapest island option, Pearl Hotel in An Thoi was $303 for the same nights.

Jan 1 is the afternoon flight, a swim and the night market in Duong Dong. Jan 2 is the An Thoi island tour with the Hon Thom cable car at 1,450,000 VND each, about $56. Jan 3 is a southern day: the Coconut Prison and, if the water looks clean, Sao and Khem beaches. Fiona Mai of Fiona Travels from Asia reminds readers that the island is bigger than it looks and a day with too many stops turns into a day in the car, so I kept it to two. Jan 4 is Ham Ninh fishing village, a fish sauce factory and an afternoon flight back for one more night at the Adora.

Leaving (Jan 5). Check out, be at Tan Son Nhat by about 14:15 for the 5:15 PM departure, and land at SFO at 6:45 PM the same calendar day.

What it will really cost

Here is the arithmetic for two travelers. Flights are $4,396 for China Airlines via Taipei, an observed Skyscanner price for the exact dates, not an airline checkout total; the same flights ran $4,392 to $4,881 across providers, and the cheapest row's carry-on status is unknown. Hotels are $1,327.71: $572 for seven Adora nights, one derived Adora night at $81.71 for Jan 4, and $674 for three Phu Quoc nights, with taxes and fees not itemized on the booking pages.

Meals are $441.33 (breakfast is included at both hotels; I used VND 600,000 for a two-person mid-range dinner from Numbeo and Slow Vietnam's casual and seafood prices at VND 25,900 to the dollar). Ground transport is $357.84, of which $220 is four domestic flight tickets that I estimated at $55 each because no Jan 1 or Jan 4 fare appeared. Activities are $330.63, including $50 for the e-visas (grouped here because the cost sheet has no separate entry line), and the reserve is $250. The total is $7,103.51.

The optional Vespa food tour from $98 each is not in that number; it would add $196. The low case of $6,281.42 uses the cheapest provider fare and Pearl Hotel for the island; the high case of $8,615.79 assumes the top of every range.

On value, I would call this conditional: you get a strong food-and-culture trip, but it runs about $104 over your ceiling in the middle case. Cutting Phu Quoc saves about $851 (net of about $383 for three more Adora nights, meals and rides) and leaves an all-city trip at about $6,253 with no beach.

Where this can go wrong

The visa timeline is the first risk. Two official pages disagree, and the fee is non-refundable, so do not pay for it until you are ready to commit to the dates.

Second, the budget has almost no slack. The airfare moved $27 per person between two reads a few minutes apart, and a rise to the airline's own price of $4,881 pushes the middle case to about $7,589.

Third, Phu Quoc in early January. The debris is seasonal, driven by the northeast monsoon according to Slow Vietnam and by tides after the flood season according to the island's own agriculture and environment department, and I cannot tell you what Jan 1 will look like. Fourth, Dec 31 to Jan 1 is the most expensive stretch of the year; domestic flights tighten around Dec 31, and a fare I could not see is the largest unpriced item.

Who should not choose this: anyone who needs a winter landscape, anyone who dislikes a long flight with a date-line jump, or a traveler who wants a long beach week without a debris risk. Fiona Mai's warning that Phu Quoc is not for people who want seclusion is fair.

The best alternative

The strongest competitor I priced is the same trip starting Dec 19: $8,725.12 in the middle case, about $1,621.61 more for four extra city days, and it relies on remote-work permission, which you have attested but I have not verified. I would not switch unless a Dec 19 fare drops under about $1,893 per person, which is the most that fits under your ceiling with every other line unchanged and well below anything I saw.

The other alternative is the same trip without Phu Quoc, spending the three beach nights in Ho Chi Minh City. It saves about $851 and brings the middle case to about $6,253, under your ceiling, at the cost of the beach. If the airfare checks at $4,881 or the island beaches look covered in debris the week before, I would switch to it.
