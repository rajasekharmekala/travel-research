Istanbul: keep the food-and-history trip, but do not force the budget

My recommendation

I would keep Istanbul in contention as a slow, one-base food-and-history trip, but I would not choose it on price yet. The most promising combination is the December 23 departure from SFO, January 5 return, and twelve nights at Ayasultan Hotel after arriving December 24. Its working budget for two is USD 7,269.16, already above your firm USD 7,000 ceiling. A lean spending scenario reaches USD 6,845.53; that is not a verified market offer. The combined high direct-reference case is USD 12,405.12, mixing Lufthansa airfare, the cancellable direct hotel rate and higher spending; it is not an upper bound. There are too many unresolved costs to rank this as a bargain.

Indian ordinary-passport holders need a Turkish visa. The official public checker says you can apply for an e-Visa, and your user-attested valid US visas may be supporting documents, but each person must finish the individual conditions privately. The current portal requires login beyond that public check. Its FAQ advises applying at least 48 hours before departure, which counts back to December 21 for the core window, not a guaranteed processing deadline. The one result that would flip my recommendation is a genuine baggage-inclusive airline-direct and tax-inclusive hotel combination that leaves enough room for the rest of the trip inside USD 7,000.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Istanbul serves food and culture far better than beach or assured winter scenery. It is a city where one afternoon can connect an imperial courtyard, a market used for ordinary shopping, and a meal whose ingredients tell you something about Anatolia. UNESCO's account of the Historic Areas explains why the skyline matters: Eastern Roman and Ottoman architecture overlap rather than sitting in separate museum districts. That gives your walking days a coherent subject, not just a string of famous buildings.

The waterfront is scenery, not a substitute for a beach holiday. December climate references describe cold coastal wind, frequent rain, and just over nine hours of daylight. I would expect to carry waterproof shoes and spend as much time warming up over soup and coffee as photographing the Bosphorus. Snow is possible, not something this itinerary earns a premium for. If what you most want is outdoor warmth, choose a different destination after its own current research; do not ask Istanbul to behave like the Riviera Maya.

I would use the core holiday window. The earlier December 19 departure is permitted by your attested remote-work arrangement, but the current SFO nonstop lead costs USD 5,127 for two before verified baggage conditions, against USD 4,071 with bags in the core-window search. Four additional hotel nights make that extension harder to justify. I have not proved every earlier routing worse, and I am not claiming remote work is independently verified. I simply would not spend more days and money to solve a problem Istanbul does not have: there is ample time in the core window for an unhurried first visit.

How the trip unfolds

Arrive and make the old city familiar

The preferred nonstop lead leaves SFO December 23 and reaches IST at 18:45 on December 24. That makes the first hotel night December 24, not December 23. I would spend that evening getting through immigration, taking the official airport coach or a licensed transfer, eating close to the hotel, and sleeping. No palace visit belongs on arrival night. The coach route serves Çatladıkapı and Eminönü; which stop is better depends on your luggage and the hotel's directions. Allow a broad traffic and airport-processing buffer rather than trusting a fixed arrival-at-hotel time.

Ayasultan is my practical first choice if its public provider room remains available. The Standard Double is modest at 17 square metres, with breakfast and a very central location. Its Booking.com page displays a 9.1/10 guest score, but that does not turn a small room into a suite. Public guest Jackie from Canada explicitly notes small rooms; I take that as useful pushback. Celal Sultan's Deluxe Double/Twin is the roomier alternative at 20 square metres, but its observed direct twelve-night total converts to about USD 2,090.60. I would not spend that difference automatically. Neither property's Chase Travel status is publicly verified; check your own portal without assuming a benefit or subtracting a credit.

December 25 is a gentle orientation through the Hippodrome and Blue Mosque area, then the Grand Bazaar if its holiday operations are confirmed. The Hippodrome helps you see the public space of Constantinople, while the Blue Mosque's İznik tilework shows a later Ottoman response to the same skyline. Visiting worship areas must yield to prayer and local visitor rules. I would not rush four paid monuments into this first full day.

December 26 belongs to Topkapı. Its courtyards are an account of how an empire organised itself: government, education, domestic life, gardens and ceremonial gates. The combined admission includes the Harem and Hagia Irene, so do not buy them again separately. Allow a substantial morning and early afternoon, then recover in Gülhane rather than adding another palace. The official palace schedule closes Tuesdays; December 26 avoids that. Outdoor courts still expose you to winter rain even when the collections are indoors.

Cross the city for food, not another checklist

The following days switch scale. December 27 is Kadıköy's food market followed by Moda. Lisa Morrow, the long-time resident behind Inside Out In Istanbul, recommends treating this area as a sequence of breakfast, food shops, coffee and a ferry home. Her description supports the slower pace I want here, but her suggestions are not December opening guarantees. Kedi's resident guide at Let's Travel to Türkiye reinforces the contrast with tourist-focused Sultanahmet: the market is more than fish, and the bookshops and cafés earn time of their own.

I would wander Güneşlibahçe's food lanes, choose lunch after looking at menus, and make Moda's waterfront the weather-dependent second chapter. Çiya is an interesting possible meal because the guide describes its regional, changing dishes; its exact bill is not established in this run. Do not substitute a made-up restaurant price for the menu-based spending scenario. If ferries are disrupted, postpone the crossing rather than make a rough-water outing a requirement.

December 28 is Galata and İstiklal on foot, with cafés and bookshops providing shelter. I would admire Galata Tower from the street before deciding whether another paid viewpoint improves the trip. December 29 then gives Istanbul Modern a proper visit and Karaköy a slower food-and-waterfront walk. Modern art is not filler: it stops the trip from presenting Istanbul only as a former imperial capital. The museum's ordinary nonresident fee is 1,000 lira; resident-only free sessions do not apply to you.

December 30 links Süleymaniye with the Spice Bazaar and Eminönü's surrounding food shops. The mosque complex is one of Sinan's great contributions to the historic skyline; the bazaar supplies the commercial counterpoint. I would look at spices, olives, cheeses and coffee rather than purchase a large souvenir basket just because it is photogenic. Food gifts brought back to the US must be declared and comply with CBP restrictions.

Keep New Year flexible, and leave room for a second look

December 31 is deliberately lighter: a short Galata exterior walk, then Pera Museum in the day, followed by an early neighborhood dinner or a separately checked venue. Pera's Ottoman and European paintings, including the collection associated with Osman Hamdi Bey's The Tortoise Trainer, invite you to think about who represented this city and for whom. I would not promise a particular canvas will be on display. A dinner cruise, gala or fireworks viewing is not included as an observed experience or cost; a compulsory holiday menu could break the budget.

January 1 stays close to the hotel. Istanbul Modern and Pera both publish New Year's Day closures, so neither is a rainy-day rescue that day. Topkapı publishes New Year as open, but since you have already visited I would not pay again unless a disruption displaced December 26. Use a short Hippodrome walk and Blue Mosque courtyard/exterior comparison, with any interior visit conditional on worship rules, then a confirmed-open meal instead. The existing itinerary remains useful if the weather is unpleasant because you can cut the walk without losing an expensive reservation.

January 2 revisits Kadıköy for Akmar Passage's bookshops and Yeldeğirmeni's murals and cafés. January 3 is Balat and the Golden Horn in daylight. The official tourism account makes the district's Jewish, Greek Orthodox and Bulgarian histories relevant, but I would photograph public streets respectfully and never assume schools, religious buildings or residential interiors are open. Slippery slopes are a reason to shorten this day.

January 4 is the final paid anchor: Hagia Sophia's designated visitor route, then a quiet second look at the old city. The current museum information page lists EUR 25 and winter hours through 19:30, but prayer restrictions, restoration access and actual visitor routing still need a near-date check. The Basilica Cistern is only an optional swap: the retrieved material contains a temporary-closure warning alongside normal hours, and live reopening was not established. I will not quietly treat it as open. January 5 is for the airport and the return flight, not one last attraction.

Where this can go wrong

The biggest risk is confusing a promising displayed price with a reproducible total. The older Istanbul report used a different return date and incorrectly began lodging before arrival; this assessment replaces those assumptions rather than claiming a measured price drop on the same trip. The new provider disagreement is still unresolved. A room's general free-cancellation label is also not permission to wait indefinitely: verify the exact time, timezone and penalty before any commitment.

Rain, wind and ferry disruption can erase an outdoor afternoon, while short daylight compresses Balat and waterfront walking. Mog and Dog Travels' account, based on living through Istanbul winters, supports the cafés-and-museums approach but also undermines the fantasy of constant outdoor sightseeing. I reject its broad winter-is-cheaper claim as a basis for your holiday fare. Your dates require their own evidence.

This is not the trip for someone who wants long beach days, guaranteed snow, a spacious resort room or a gala every evening. It also is not yet ready for nonrefundable spending.
