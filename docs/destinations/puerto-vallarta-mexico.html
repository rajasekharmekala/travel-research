Puerto Vallarta: stay central, protect the return, and resolve the room contract

My recommendation

I would use Puerto Vallarta as a single-base beach-and-food trip from December 23 to January 5, not a resort-hopping circuit. My first choice is Joint Boutique Hotel's interior king junior suite, provided you are comfortable with a room without an outside view and get its holiday cancellation terms clarified. I would pair it with Alaska's morning nonstop outbound and same-day LAX return only after the 1h42 US-entry/bag-recheck connection is confirmed workable and protected, and the hotel clarifies its holiday contract in writing. If either fails, this combination is not ready to choose. The checked nonstop fallback is a $7,069.77 spending scenario, already $69.77 above your ceiling; no automatically viable under-ceiling replacement is evidenced.

The scenario-based planning case is $6,579.77 for two, including $250 of unsupported ground spending and a $600 unsupported reserve—not a priced insurance policy. The displayed subtotal BEFORE bags is two $1,002 fares plus $1,794.28 hotel after derived FX conversion: $3,798.28. Adding $180 policy-derived bags makes the fare-plus-hotel subtotal $3,978.28; neither subtotal includes the rest of the trip. Menu/activity references and derived bag costs bring the referenced subtotal to $5,729.77; $850 of unsupported scenarios then brings it to $6,579.77. The combined scenarios run from $6,193.31 to $8,310.24; this is not a guaranteed market range. The upper case breaks your firm $7,000 ceiling, so I would keep the itinerary conditional rather than describe it as an established bargain. Most importantly, ground transport contains an explicitly unsupported allowance. I cannot honestly rank total-trip value until that gap is priced. The fact that the middle scenario fits is useful, but it is not proof that every version fits.

For an Indian passport holder with a valid, unexpired US visa throughout the visit, the Mexican consulate in San Francisco explicitly says a separate Mexican tourist visa is not needed. Both travelers' valid US stamps and US re-entry are user-attested, not inspected. The consulate also cautions that carriers may apply additional passport-validity rules; confirm the actual Indian-passport requirement with the airline. This exemption has no advance Mexican visa application; if either document does not qualify, the consular fallback has an unpublished processing time and needs an appointment. I would change the recommendation if a satisfactory central room or baggage-inclusive itinerary pushes the verified total above $7,000, or if the entry exemption does not apply.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Puerto Vallarta serves beach, food, culture and accessible nature better than winter scenery. You get the sea and a walkable town rather than a beach resort with nothing beyond its gate. The attraction for me is the combination: a morning spent among orchid collections, an evening on Basilio Badillo, a whale boat on another day, and enough unstructured afternoons to return to a beach you actually like. I would not sell this as a snow alternative with equivalent scenery. It is a deliberate choice of warm coast over alpine winter.

Seasonal averages are favorable but more specific than the tourism board's broad winter description. WeatherSpark's monthly averages show December highs/lows of 82°F/64°F and January 81°F/62°F, with average water temperatures of 79°F and 77°F respectively. Rainfall averages are 0.6 inches in December and 1.1 inches in January. Those are regional historical summaries, not forecasts for your dates. The shortest daylight period is about 10h53 near the solstice. I would do the hill and garden early and leave beach sunsets as optional endings, not the reason to delay a road return.

Your earlier December 19 window is usable under the user-attested remote-work permission, but I do not see enough upside to choose it here. The Booking interior-suite reference increases from $1,766 to $2,264 before 21% taxes: $498 more before tax, or $602.58 after tax for four nights, on that same provider. Food and transport also grow, and the early SFO nonstop-outbound headline was higher. Cheap two-stop overnight leads exist, but their return and fare terms were not completed. The longer window is an option for more time, not a demonstrated saving.

How the trip unfolds

Arrive directly into the town you will actually use

On December 23 I would take Alaska AS754, leaving SFO at 7:04 AM and arriving at PVR at 12:49 PM. Its displayed elapsed time is 3h45. That is an early start, but it avoids a connection and gives you daylight for immigration and the transfer. I would use an authorized airport provider and go straight to Joint at the Insurgentes bridge. Allow 30–60 minutes for the road transfer as a holiday planning buffer, not a promised drive time. Check-in is 3 PM, so luggage storage needs confirmation if you arrive sooner.

Joint is not my choice because it has a card benefit. Public Chase pages did not establish that it, Posada de Roger or Rivera del Rio is in The Edit or the logged-in travel portal. All remain unverified; no credit or breakfast benefit is deducted. Joint wins on the location and the available room cost. Its own site places it beside the Cuale, with coworking and a gym. The inspected interior junior suite has no promised view, and I would ask about ventilation, air conditioning, noise and natural light before accepting thirteen nights there. A kitchenette belongs to the more expensive studio, not automatically to this room.

Julie Schwietert Collazo's older AFAR neighborhood piece emphasizes cobblestones, cafés and beach proximity. That supports the walkable-base idea, but its age means I would not borrow its safety assurance or hours as current facts. The historical report's Comfort Inn near the airport would make arrival easier but evenings more transport-dependent. For thirteen nights, I would prioritize the place you leave the hotel to enjoy, not the last few minutes of an airport transfer.

Christmas stays deliberately simple

December 24 is Cuale Island, the public market and the parish/plaza area. The island divides the town and its cultural center explains why this is more than a souvenir stop. The archaeology museum is described as free by the tourism board, but its holiday opening is not confirmed. If it is shut, the outdoor island and plaza remain the plan; I would not invent a museum reservation.

December 25 is Los Muertos Beach and its illuminated sail-shaped pier, reached on foot from the base. The beach is the city's social shoreline: families, vendors, boats and restaurant terraces, not a quiet private cove. Plan a walk and sand time first; swimming depends on local warnings and conditions. No lounger, beach-club minimum spend or Christmas meal is included as a confirmed offer. The botanical garden is closed on Christmas Day, so it is not a fallback that day.

Meagan Drillinger, writing in Mexico News Daily in November 2025, makes the useful distinction between local religious customs and the commercial waterfront celebrations. Her account also notes crowded New Year's waterfronts and holiday dining demand. It makes me cautious about promising a public spectacle on Christmas Eve. The Guadalupe processions she describes run December 1–12, before either authorized departure. I would not move the trip earlier simply to imply you will see them.

Spend on the bay and the garden, not a daily tour

December 26 is the whale excursion, with a marina walk afterward rather than a second major outing. Ecotours' December 26, 8:30 AM schedule showed a 12 PM return and two adult tickets at $131.59 each including taxes and fees. I have also allowed the $5-per-person port fee stated on the operator page, pending confirmation that the ticket display has not already included it. The earlier one-person screen showed a standby/minimum-four-participant notice; the later two-ticket screen did not show it. That is not proof the departure will operate. Ask whether the minimum is met and what happens if weather cancels the trip.

Aga Moe's review, reproduced on the operator's page with a relative date, praises the guide and the distance kept from whales. I read that as an argument for the educational small-boat experience, not for getting closer to wildlife. It is also provider-hosted review evidence, not an independent safety certification. Sightings remain uncertain. If rough seas or health restrictions make the boat unsuitable, switch to the marina and a town food day rather than chase a replacement boat at any price.

I would use December 27 for a slow Centro/plaza circuit and December 28 for Vallarta Botanical Gardens. The garden's detailed official page is more reliable than a generic statement that it opens seven days in winter: it explicitly excludes December 25 and January 1. General admission is MXN 300 per adult. The El Tuito bus reference is MXN 50 per person each way, approximately 45 minutes, leaving every half hour. The total admission-plus-bus reference for two is MXN 800. That is much less than the garden's MXN 1,200-per-person shuttle package, but it buys less convenience and is subject to the actual holiday service.

At the garden, I would split time between the Orchid & Native Plants Conservatory and a short forest trail. The collections preserve western Mexican biodiversity, including orchids, oaks and magnolias; the second visit lets you see the surrounding ecosystem rather than just glasshouses. Allow about five hours on site as a planning choice. River swimming is explicitly at your own risk and is not necessary to make the day worthwhile. Rain makes the trail the first thing to drop, not the whole visit.

Leave room for repetition and a considered New Year

The remainder is deliberately paced around Gringo Gulch, the Cuale cultural center, a food tour, Los Muertos, the lookout and galleries. This is thirteen nights with return visits and recovery time, not thirteen distinct attraction days. The repeated beach/island walks are optional rests that preserve flexibility; skip them rather than invent a paid outing just to fill a box. On December 30 I would take the Original Downtown food tour if its calendar confirms service. The public starting price is $59 each, not an exact-date total. It earns its time by introducing regional dishes and their context, then helps you decide what to eat independently. I have not removed a meal from the food basket: the savings from substituting tastings for lunch are upside, not an assumption.

For New Year's Eve I would keep the daytime close to home and choose a waterfront stroll you can leave easily. The municipal festival article captured is explicitly about 2025–26, so its lineup and free activities are not a 2026–27 promise. A reserved dinner is a separate check; Makal's regular menu is not a New Year's menu. Its normal closure is Monday, so I place the optional regular-menu dinner on Tuesday, December 29, rather than Monday, January 4.

January 1 is a recovery day on the beach and Cuale Island, not the closed garden. January 2 can include the Saturday market if this season's holiday operation is confirmed. January 3 is the Cerro de la Cruz climb in morning light, with a Malecón walk afterward; skip the steep steps if knees, heat or rain argue against it. January 4 is galleries and Basilio Badillo with packing time. Return January 5 through LAX: 3:02 PM from PVR, 1h42 connection, SFO at 7:35 PM, displayed 6h33. Immigration and bag recheck at the first US airport make that connection a real risk to verify, not a carefree shortcut.

Where this can go wrong

The largest avoidable mistake is paying for the wrong room expectation. Thirteen nights in an interior suite are reasonable only if you actually want it. Joint's early-rate terms suggest limited cancellation, while its general page says holiday reservations can incur the full stay. Booking says cancellation is free before December 22 for the core reference and charges the full reservation inside one day. These statements need channel-specific written clarification. The cheaper direct amount is not automatically the best risk-adjusted amount.

The second mistake is treating a pretty beach as a safe swimming beach or a seasonal whale trip as guaranteed. Follow local flags and operator restrictions. FCDO's current country advice warns about crime affecting major tourist destinations, including Puerto Vallarta, and unpredictable sea conditions. Its regional warning areas are not a city-wide safety verdict; nor does a tourist-zone address remove risk. Use reputable transport, avoid isolated night walks, keep valuables restrained and do not extend the trip into remote inland drives just to fill a day.

The third is overbuying holiday experiences. A gala dinner, beach club, extra boat day and nonstop premium can jointly erase the budget margin. I would dislike this trip if I wanted silence every evening, a guaranteed sea view, snow, or a different major attraction every day. Someone who likes returning to a familiar café and beach will get more from this base than someone who needs a busy touring circuit.
