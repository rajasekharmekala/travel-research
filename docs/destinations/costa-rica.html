Advisor memo

Costa Rica as a Liberia loop costs about $6,756 for two, under your ceiling, if your US visa stamps count for entry

My recommendation

I would take Costa Rica as a Liberia loop: fly San Francisco to Liberia on American via Dallas, spend five nights around La Fortuna for the volcano, the hot springs and the rainforest, then eight nights on the Guanacaste coast at Playas del Coco, and fly home from the same airport on Jan 5. Plan on about $6,756 for the two of you in the middle case, with a realistic range of roughly $5,000 to $8,900 depending on airfare after bag fees, hotel tax and how many paid extras you add. That is $244 under your firm $7,000 ceiling and $756 over the $6,000 target in the trip settings.

Entry is the one-line verdict you need first, and it is unresolved: Indian passports are in Costa Rica's consular-visa group, and the only way around a visa is a qualifying multiple-entry US visa. The Costa Rican embassy page says any multiple-entry US visa category counts with one day of validity left; the Indian embassy's advisory says only B1/B2, D or C1/D with six months left, and an H-1B is neither B1/B2 nor D. Your stamps are user-attested as valid through Jan 5, which proves neither reading, so a written answer from a Costa Rican consulate is required before any purchase.

What would flip my recommendation is the stamps. If the Costa Rican consulate says an H-1B stamp does not count, or one stamp expires before about June 23, 2027, this becomes a consular-visa trip with an unpublished timeline and I would pause it.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Your list is beach, food and culture, winter scenery, nature and value. This route serves nature and beach best: volcano and rainforest days in the first half, then the driest, sunniest, calmest-sea coast in Costa Rica in the second. It serves value well because both hotel stays I found are cheap for the holiday week. It trades away winter scenery entirely, which no tropical trip gives you, and it gives you less food and culture than Mexico City, Lisbon or Morocco would, because the food here is excellent but humble: casados at family sodas, fresh fish at a harbour.

The window matters. Dec 23 to Jan 5 is Costa Rica's busiest stretch. A Costa Rica Day Trip guide puts La Fortuna rooms at $200 to $280 a night over Christmas against $130 to $180 in early December, and Two Weeks in Costa Rica says peak crowds run Dec 20 to Jan 5 while most businesses stay open. The rates I saw are below those benchmarks but flagged "1 left", so the window shapes the risk more than the price.

How the trip unfolds

Arrival (Dec 23). The flight leaves San Francisco at 12:31 AM, so you are at the airport the night of Dec 22. After Dallas you land in Liberia at 1:00 PM and take a private minibus for about 3.5 hours to La Fortuna ($300; the shared shuttles listed run at 09:20 and 1:00 PM, which you cannot catch). Both flight legs are under 11 hours, well inside your 24-hour cap.

La Fortuna, five nights at Arenal Rústic Lodge. I would book the one-bedroom chalet with breakfast at $575 for the five nights, observed for the exact dates; Casa Macaw at $316 (9.0 from 271 reviews, free cancellation, no breakfast) is the cheaper fallback and Cabañas Sueños del Arenal at $456 the other. Christmas Eve is the waterfall in the afternoon, about 500 steps down and back up, and a soda dinner next to the park; an independent traveler on Nomadic Backpacker warns that the steps and the heat are no joke and the current is very strong. Christmas Day is the volcano. Arenal Volcano National Park opens at 8:00 AM every day including holidays, and I would walk the Las Coladas trail to the 1992 lava flows first, then Los Miradores on the lake side after the clouds come in. Dec 26 is the Mistico hanging bridges in the morning and the Ecotermales night pass at 7:00 PM, which Two Weeks in Costa Rica says sells out in December and should be booked at least two weeks ahead. Dec 27 is the Peñas Blancas safari float and the sodas. I left Río Celeste out on purpose: a local operator warns it turns brown for about 24 hours after heavy rain and has a 2:00 PM entry cutoff, and it is the swap for a rainy day, at $185 for a private shuttle plus $12 plus tax admission.

Guanacaste, eight nights at Hotel M&M Beach House in Playas del Coco. On Dec 28 a shared shuttle takes 3.5 to 4.5 hours to the coast. The room is beachfront, $800 for eight nights, with free cancellation. I will be honest about Coco: a beach guide on Costa Rica Spirit calls the town beach one of the country's most overrated and says the value is access, with the harbour, the dive shops and a road of about 35 minutes to the airport, per that one guide. That is why the plan uses Coco as a base for the better places: Playa Ocotal for snorkelling and Playa Hermosa for a cleaner bay, a day at Rincón de la Vieja (Las Pailas and the La Cangreja waterfall, $24.95 each), a sunset sail and snorkel from the harbour on New Year's Eve for about $95 each, and a New Year's Eve dinner with fireworks on the beach. If you want more beach variety, swap a spare day for Conchal and Flamingo, the white-sand beaches the same guide recommends.

Departure (Jan 5). A taxi to Liberia of about 35 minutes (one beach guide), a 2:00 PM flight via Dallas and a 10:17 PM landing in San Francisco, which means you are home the same day rather than at 12:37 AM on Jan 6.

None of the four stays I list appears on Chase's public Costa Rica Edit pages (I checked Chase's guide and a third-party list of all ten Edit properties today); the Chase Travel portal needs your login, so look there, but I would not let a card match outrank a room this good for the price.

Hotels $1,375.00: $575 plus $800, both exact-date Booking.com totals. Meals $928.00: eight breakfasts at $8, fourteen lunches at $10 and thirteen dinners at $20 per person, from published price ranges. Local transport $935.00: a private LIR minibus ($300), a shared shuttle ($190), airport taxi, La Fortuna taxis and a day driver to Rincón de la Vieja. Activities $634.98: waterfall $40, Arenal $33.90, Ecotermales $97.18, Mistico $56, safari float $168, sunset sail $190 and Rincón $49.90. A $300 reserve covers the first-bag fee and surprises; it is a reserve, not an insurance premium.

The swing factors are the airfare after the bag fee and whether Booking.com totals already include the 13 percent sales tax. The low case of about $5,004 uses the cheaper return that lands at 12:37 AM, cheaper rooms and no paid extras. The high case of about $8,876 stacks every upgrade and is not the plan. On value, the Liberia loop costs about $103 more than the Avianca route as priced and about $423 more if both travel carry-on only (Avianca's total includes a checked bag each way, about $320; American's quote excludes bags, so its bag fee widens the gap). It wins on risk, not price: it carries no known single-point price risk.

Where this can go wrong

The entry stamps are the biggest risk, as above. Next is the American fare: the airline's own site blocked me with a bot challenge, so the $2,583 is an aggregator quote until you recheck it on aa.com in a normal browser and see the first-bag fee. Hotel inventory is thin: both rooms showed "We have 1 left" and the platform said 80 to 95 percent of rated places were unavailable for these dates. The 12:31 AM departure is a real fatigue cost: about 17 hours door to door on arrival day, including the 3.5-hour drive. Rain is a small risk in La Fortuna (about 220 mm in December) and almost nothing in Guanacaste, but afternoon winds can cancel boats.

This trip is wrong for someone who wants a food- or culture-led holiday, who dislikes long shuttles on the second day, or who wants a postcard beach: Coco is a working harbour town, and the lovely beaches are a drive away.

The best alternative

The strongest competitor is the San José, La Fortuna and Manuel Antonio route on Avianca. Avianca's own checkout showed $2,377.50 for two with checked bags on the 13:15 Dec 23 and 15:10 Jan 5 flights through San Salvador, and with Vista del Pacifico at $969 in Manuel Antonio the total is $6,653, $103 less than the Liberia loop as priced (about $423 less if both travel carry-on only). It looks better on paper because Manuel Antonio has the more famous wildlife. I rejected it for three reasons. First, El Salvador's airport authority publishes a US$1,000 plus VAT per-passenger tariff for listed transit nationalities billed to airlines; a third-party guide says India is on the list and the official list was not readable, so the exposure is up to about $2,260 for two and would push the total to about $8,913. Second, Manuel Antonio National Park is closed every Tuesday, sells timed tickets online only and sells out three to four weeks ahead in December, and Dec 29 and Jan 5 are Tuesdays. Third, the shuttle from La Fortuna takes 4h43 to 7 hours.

The result that would make me switch is a written confirmation from Avianca that no El Salvador transit charge is billed to an Indian passport on that routing; then it becomes a close, fair call, and the extra Manuel Antonio wildlife could win it.
