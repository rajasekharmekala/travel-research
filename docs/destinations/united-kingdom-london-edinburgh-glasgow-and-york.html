United Kingdom: London, Edinburgh, Glasgow and York

The London, Edinburgh, Glasgow-day and York loop is the right shape for 22 December to 5 January: it respects the Christmas rail shutdown by moving north on 27 December, puts JORVIK and the walls before York's January closures, and spends the shortest days indoors. Value-first stays cut lodging to about USD 2,440. But priced on 28 October the trip costs about USD 11,502 for two (USD 8,769 in the leanest sourced scenario), against a firm USD 7,000 ceiling; flights and hotels alone are about USD 6,802. It is a well-supported no at this budget, not a verdict on the places. UK visas are required (GBP 135 each, about 3 weeks, last safe date 3 November 2026).

Advisor memo

London, Edinburgh, Glasgow and York is the right UK trip for 22 December to 5 January, and cheaper stays still leave it far over your USD 7,000 ceiling

My recommendation

I would not book this trip at your USD 7,000 ceiling, and re-pricing the hotels did not change that. The loop I built, London for Christmas, Edinburgh through Hogmanay with a day in Glasgow, then York and back to London, comes to about USD 11,502 for the two of you. The leanest version I could source, with Air Canada's one-stop out and two-stop return and the cheapest confirmed stays, is about USD 8,769. The United nonstop flights alone are USD 3,882 as observed (Basic, carry-on only) or USD 4,362 with the outbound Standard fare, and up to about USD 4,842 if the return leg is upgraded too, which I assumed and did not observe; Air Canada at USD 2,750 is the cheaper lean option. Thirteen nights of value-first hotels are about USD 2,440, down from about USD 3,447 for the mid-range hotels I priced first.

The trip itself is good. It is strong on food and culture, which carry the most weight in your preferences, and on winter city scenery. It has no beach and almost no nature, so it trades those two away entirely. The price is the problem, and the dates are why: Christmas week and Hogmanay are the most expensive and darkest days of the year to be in Britain.

Entry is the most urgent item. You both need a UK Standard Visitor visa (GBP 135 each, a decision that gov.uk says usually comes within three weeks). The last safe date to apply is 3 November 2026. That is 22 December minus 49 days: the 21-day usual decision time, a 14-day wait I assumed for a biometrics slot (appointment availability is not public) and a 14-day margin for the holidays. With no margin at all the cut-off is 17 November. Either way, it is about a week away. Apply this week and book nothing non-refundable until the visas are back, which matters here because the cheapest hotel rates are non-refundable. The one thing that would flip my answer is you raising the ceiling, which only you can do.

Why this trip fits the two of you

Food and culture are what London, Edinburgh and York are best at, and Glasgow adds a free museum day. You get a theatre night on Boxing Day, a pub lunch under the Castle, Bettys in York, and museums that are free, such as the National Museum of Scotland, the British Museum, Kelvingrove and the Railway Museum. Winter scenery shows up as the Edinburgh skyline, the city walls in York and the walk up Glasgow's Necropolis, all done in the roughly seven hours of daylight there are.

It serves nature and beach badly. Met Office averages put the highs at about 7 degrees Celsius in all four places, with 12 to 17 rainy days a month and under 60 hours of December sunshine. Value is the other preference it fails, and I will be plain about that below.

Your dates shape the whole route. You land at Heathrow at 7:25 AM on 23 December on United's nonstop, and there is no evening return: the latest United nonstop leaves Heathrow at 1:45 PM on 5 January and reaches San Francisco at 4:55 PM.

How the trip unfolds

You said the hotels are only for sleeping, so I picked the best-value stay in each place, down to a Booking score of 8.0 and accepting farther-out locations. The scores here are 8.1 to 8.5.

London, 23 to 26 December (four nights at Otherwander Soho Pod). Christmas week closes London more than people expect. The National Gallery, British Museum, V&A and Churchill War Rooms are all shut 24 to 26 December, Borough Market is closed 25 to 27 December, and on Christmas Day there is no Tube, bus, tram or train. That is why I chose a Soho capsule hotel: it is 13 minutes on foot to Trafalgar Square, scores 8.5 from about 1,058 reviews, and costs USD 419 before VAT, about USD 503 with VAT. The catch is a capsule bed and a shared bathroom. If you want a private bathroom, Crestfield Hotel by King's Cross scores 8.1 with a cleanliness sub-score of 8.6 and costs about USD 109 more before VAT at the public rate (about USD 131 with VAT).

So the days are built around what stays open. On the 23rd you land, drop bags, and eat at Borough Market, open 10:00 to 18:00, then see the Christmas lights at Covent Garden. The 24th is Tate Modern and Winter Wonderland in Hyde Park. On Christmas Day I would watch the Serpentine Swimming Club race for the Peter Pan Cup, held since 1864, and walk to lunch at Thai Square on Trafalgar Square, which says it is open 365 days. Tracy Collins of London Travel Planning says "the city stops in a way that few visitors are ready for", and she is right. Boxing Day is Sky Garden if you can book a free slot and a West End show; Les Miserables tickets start from GBP 25 on the official site, and I budgeted GBP 32 each, though I could not confirm a 26 December performance.

The move north, Sunday 27 December. National Rail shows no King's Cross to Edinburgh service on Saturday the 26th (most operators are shut, with a very limited service still to be confirmed), and Monday the 28th is the substituted bank holiday, so Sunday is the day. I priced the Lumo 11:20 from King's Cross, arriving Edinburgh at 15:32, at GBP 145.80 for two, with LNER's 08:30 at GBP 285.00 as the fallback. One caution: a Telegraph travel writer expects East Coast Main Line closures and replacement buses over Christmas 2026. I found no notice for the 27th, but I did not verify it line by line, so check National Rail before you book.

Edinburgh, 27 December to 2 January (six nights at West Coates Mansion). This is where I saved the most. West Coates Mansion in Murrayfield scores 8.2 from 447 reviews, with a 9.1 location score, and costs USD 1,293 before tax, about USD 1,606 all-in, against about USD 2,147 for the Moxy I priced first, a saving of about USD 541. The cost is distance: it is about 1.4 miles from the centre, a mile on foot from Haymarket station, and a bus stop is outside. The festive bus timetable is unpublished, so on 31 December and 1 January you may be walking home.

Daylight here runs 08:43 to 15:43 at the end of December. The 28th is the Castle (GBP 23.50 each online, book ahead) and Holyroodhouse (GBP 22 each), and on the 29th you do the National Museum of Scotland and the Torchlight Procession, which gathers at The Meadows at 18:30 and starts at 19:30. On the 31st you spend the day in the Botanic Garden and decide about the Street Party, which I explain below. On New Year's Day nothing starts early: no ScotRail trains, the Castle opens at 11:00, and the free First Footin' music trail fills the afternoon.

Glasgow, 30 December (a day trip). You asked to include Glasgow, and I priced it as a base first. Glasgow hotels were about USD 475 to 725 all-in for the six nights (13 hotels priced, scores 7.2 to 8.7) with almost no Hogmanay premium, but ScotRail runs no trains on 1 January and ends earlier on New Year's Eve, so a Glasgow base could not reach the Edinburgh events. So Glasgow is a day trip instead. The train takes 44 minutes at best and about an hour on average, at GBP 16.80 return each. The Hunterian is officially open on the 30th, so I would do Kelvingrove, lunch, the Hunterian, and Glasgow Cathedral and the Necropolis before dusk. Glasgow Life has not yet published its 2026/27 festive museum hours (its undated festive page has weekdays that match 2025/26), so confirm them first.

The Hogmanay Street Party is the one place I would save money. A third-party price puts tickets at GBP 39 each, and the traveler reports I read are split. One Mumsnet poster calls it paying to stand on a road. TripSCOT's Gary says Calton Hill, which is free, is about 70 percent of the experience. And wareontheglobe reports most Hogmanay events were cancelled for wind in 2024/25. I would watch from Calton Hill.

York, 2 to 4 January (two nights at YHA York). The 08:00 train gets you to York at 10:14 for GBP 69.60 for two. YHA York has a private ensuite room for USD 144 before VAT, about USD 173 all-in, a 15-minute riverside walk to the Minster, about USD 37 below the cheapest Moxy queen and about USD 70 below the Moxy twin on a free-cancel rate. Do JORVIK first, because it is open to 3 January and shut for maintenance from 4 January, and walk the city walls before dusk locks the gates at about 15:30. Sunday is the National Railway Museum, then York Minster in its short Sunday window of 12:45 to 14:30, and Bettys. The York Trip guide says "January is York's quietest month by most measures", which is why I picked it as the fourth stop.

Back to London, 4 to 5 January. The 09:31 train arrives at 11:32 (GBP 57.60 for two). That afternoon you do the British Museum and Churchill War Rooms, the two big sights that were closed over Christmas, and sleep one night at Crestfield, two minutes from King's Cross, at about USD 158. On the 5th you take the Piccadilly line to Heathrow Terminal 2 for the 1:45 PM flight.

Where this can go wrong

Weather comes first. Wind cancelled Edinburgh Hogmanay events in 2024/25, and Cat VH on the Rick Steves forum calls late December "one of the worst times to visit", wet and windy with about seven hours of daylight. Closures second: Christmas Day has no transport, and Edinburgh and Glasgow museums have unpublished festive hours. Third is the rail: a Telegraph writer expects closures and strikes, and Advance fares can sell out, in which case the LNER Anytime single on 27 December was GBP 409.60 (valid only via York).

Cancellation exposure is lopsided. The value hotel rates are non-refundable, and United Basic Economy is non-refundable with no changes; only the Flexible fare (USD 2,403 per person on the outbound page) is refundable. That is why nothing should be booked until the visas are decided. The flexible hotel rates run 20 to 30 percent higher.

Who should not choose this trip: anyone who wants warmth, a beach or hiking, anyone who needs a private bathroom or a bed they can stretch out in every night, and anyone who cannot tolerate dark afternoons and rain.

The best alternative

The strongest alternative is the same loop at the lean prices, USD 8,769 and still over your ceiling. I also priced other departure airports. From San Jose, a Delta and Virgin one-stop with a two-stop return was USD 1,454 per person, about USD 974 below the San Francisco nonstop for two, but it lands at 11:35 AM and the return takes 19 hours; Oakland had nothing under 24 hours each way that beat San Francisco. Even the San Jose saving leaves the trip far above your ceiling.

I looked at Glasgow as a base and at swapping York for the Lake District, about 2.5 hours from Edinburgh by TransPennine Express, but January there averages 5 degrees Celsius, about 20 wet days and 8 hours of light. I did not price it. Bath is blocked by Paddington engineering until 4 January.

I would switch to a different trip entirely if the USD 7,000 ceiling stays firm, and I would come back to this one only if you raise it: the fare and hotel changes I found, even together with the San Jose option, cannot close a gap of roughly USD 1,769 to 4,502.

Application steps
  1. Confirm on GOV.UK that a Standard Visitor visa applies to each traveler (India is on the visa national list) and apply no earlier than 3 months before travel (from 22 September 2026, so the window is open).
  2. Complete one online application per traveler on GOV.UK: travel dates, where you will stay, expected trip cost, home address and time lived there, income, parents, and any other details the form requests; pay GBP 135 each.
  3. Book the biometrics appointment during the application at a VFS Global centre (San Francisco or San Jose Premium) and attend with the passport, appointment confirmation and the documents the centre lists; the passport is returned the same day.
  4. Wait for the decision by email: gov.uk says decisions usually take 3 weeks for applications outside the UK; the visa is a digital eVisa accessed through a UKVI account. Do not buy non-refundable travel before the decision.
  5. If eligible at application time and the 3-week window is too tight, add priority (GBP 500 each) or super priority (GBP 1,000 each) service.
