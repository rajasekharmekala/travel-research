Advisor memo

Colombia works as Bogota then Cartagena, but only if your visa stamps and the San Salvador connection check out

My recommendation

I would go to Colombia as two bases, Bogota then Cartagena, but only if two questions come back clean first. The point estimate is about $7,458 for the two of you, with a plausible range of roughly $6,107 to $10,597. That means the low end fits under your USD 7,000 ceiling and the middle does not. Entry is visa-free for Indian passport holders only if each US visa stamp is valid for more than six months when you land. The flip condition is simple: if either stamp is short, or the San Salvador connection carries a transit fee for you, I would drop this trip.

Why this trip fits the two of you

You asked for food and culture first, beach second, winter scenery and nature after that, and value overall. Colombia serves the first two well. Bogota gives you La Candelaria, Plaza de Bolivar and the Monserrate view, at over 2,500 meters, where December evenings are cool, around 10 C, per the monthly averages. Cartagena gives you the Walled City, Getsemani and a dry, hot week at 30 to 32 C. It trades away winter scenery almost entirely, and value suffers because December 23 to January 5 is the peak of the year for rooms.

How the trip unfolds

Arrival and Bogota (Dec 23 to 27). The flight I would price is Avianca via San Salvador, leaving SFO at 1:15 PM and landing in Bogota about 2 AM on Dec 24. That is why I put the Dec 23 night on the booking. I would stay at Holiday Inn Express & Suites Zona G in Chapinero (8.7 on Booking.com from 961 reviews, $328 for four nights with breakfast). I have not verified its Chase Travel status, so check the Chase portal yourselves.

Christmas Eve is a walk in La Candelaria and an early dinner. Christmas Day is for a long lunch, because I could not verify museum or sight hours on the holiday. Dec 26 is Monserrate. The blogger at She Wanders Abroad found the cable car ran after noon while a funicular ran before, and paid about 23,500 COP round trip, which is about $6. She also says Bogota sits above 2,500 meters, so go slowly the first day.

Cartagena (Dec 27 to Jan 4). A JetSMART flight at 11:00 AM on Dec 27 takes about 1h37 and the pair of you pay about $680 round trip on the Skyscanner snapshot. For the stay I would book Hampton by Hilton in Bocagrande (8.7 from 1,371 reviews, $1,747 for eight nights with breakfast, 150 feet from the beach) if you want a beach and a pool, or Casa Pizarro in Getsemani ($2,387, 8.9 from 860 reviews) if you want atmosphere and can spend more.

The days that matter are the Walled City and Castillo San Felipe on Dec 28, a Rosario Islands boat day on Dec 29 and a quiet New Year's Eve with a reserved dinner. Our Escape Clause writes that the beaches within walking distance are not the Caribbean paradise people imagine, and that the better beaches need a boat. I agree: do not skip the island day. Blue Apple Beach, an island operator, says Christmas through New Year is the best weather of the year and the worst availability, and advises booking boats early.

Departure (Jan 4 to 5). The return flight leaves Bogota at 4:35 AM, so you fly back from Cartagena on Jan 4 at 5:29 PM and sleep one night in Bogota (priced at the Zona G hotel rate; a hotel nearer the airport was not searched). It is a rough exit and I would pay more to avoid it if a better schedule appears.

What it will really cost

Flights are $3,668 for two: Avianca $2,988 plus JetSMART $680, both aggregator snapshots, not airline-direct totals, because the Avianca and United direct pages failed. Hotels are $2,157: Bogota $328, Cartagena $1,747 and one derived airport-night at $82. Meals outside breakfast are $928, ground transport $172 and activities $178, all reference estimates. A 5% reserve of $355 brings it to $7,458.

Low case about $6,107 uses the cheapest Avianca schedule (14h15 outbound, two stops back) and a deal-priced Cartagena hotel; the high case of about $10,597 uses Copa via Panama and a boutique hotel. The swing factor is the flight. Google Flights showed the cheapest Avianca schedule at $2,429, about $290 under the Skyscanner $2,719 for the same carrier.

Against the alternative of flying straight to Cartagena for 13 nights, this is cheaper by about $736 and gives two cities. On experience per dollar it wins, though neither is cheap.

Where this can go wrong

The biggest unknown is the El Salvador connection. Dinero HN, quoting the port authority, reports a $1,000 plus 13% VAT tariff for transit passengers of 57 countries including India, paid through the airline. I could not confirm whether a US-visa holder is exempt. If it applies to both of you on both connections, the cost is roughly $4,520 and the trip is dead; Copa via Panama avoids San Salvador at about $3,797.

Other risks are the 2 AM arrival, the 4:35 AM departure, peak New Year crowds and heat. Allinmap says December crowd levels run high to very high, accommodation costs rise 30 to 70 percent over low season and flights 20 to 50 percent. Who should not choose this: anyone who dislikes humidity, crowds or very early flights.

The best alternative

Fly straight to Cartagena on American via Miami (about $3,485 for two) and stay 13 nights. It removes the domestic flight and the 4:35 AM exit and puts you at a hotel the same evening you land. The cost is about $8,194 against $7,458, and you lose Bogota. I would switch to it only if an airline-direct fare for SFO-CTG comes in below about $3,000 for two.

What to check next

1. Look at each US visa stamp and confirm it has more than six months' validity on Dec 24, 2026. If not, plan on a consular visa instead, because the first Colombian visa must be applied for abroad. 2. Ask the airline, not a blog, whether Indian passport holders with a US visa pay the El Salvador transit tariff on the Avianca routing. If yes, price Copa via Panama instead. 3. Re-quote Avianca and JetSMART on their own sites for two adults on the exact dates, with bags. 4. Re-check the two hotels on their own sites for cancellation terms and total price. 5. Check official hours for Monserrate and the Gold Museum on Dec 24, 25 and Jan 1.

Entry and transit

Visa-free entry applies to Indian nationals only with a US or Schengen visa valid for more than six months at arrival; otherwise a consular visa is required. The Avianca routings connect in San Salvador.

Colombia's foreign ministry says the visa exemption applies to Indian nationals who prove on arrival a permanent residence permit or a visa valid for more than six months issued by the United States or a Schengen country.
